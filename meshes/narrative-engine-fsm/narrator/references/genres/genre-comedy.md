## Genre Identity

These are conventions, not commandments. Know the expectations so you can meet or subvert them intentionally.

Comedy typically lives in the gap between expectation and reality—the moment the brain catches the mismatch and releases it as laughter. The goal often isn't just jokes; it's creating conditions where humor emerges from character, situation, and the perfect wrong word at the perfect wrong time.

**Core tensions:**
- Order vs. chaos (and chaos usually wins)
- Dignity vs. humiliation
- Intelligence vs. absurdity
- Control vs. catastrophe
- What we pretend to be vs. what we are

**Comedy sub-genres:**

**Farce**
- Mistaken identities, physical chaos, escalating disasters
- Logic within madness—the situation is absurd, reactions tend to be real
- Doors, timing, pants (often optional)

**Satire**
- Comedy typically carries a target—institutions, behaviors, beliefs
- Laughter often functions as scalpel
- Usually requires understanding what it mocks to mock it well

**Parody**
- Often involves loving (or savage) imitation of specific works or genres
- Typically requires audience familiarity with source
- Often works best when it reveals truths about the original

**Romantic Comedy**
- Tends to be a love story where obstacles become comedic
- Often features misunderstandings, timing disasters, worthy adversaries
- The couple typically works best when they're funny together, not just near funny situations

**Dark Comedy**
- Often involves laughing at the abyss
- Tends to play death, failure, existential dread for laughs
- Usually requires precise calibration; too dark risks killing the comedy, too light risks betraying the dark

**Absurdist Comedy**
- Tends to break logic entirely; the nonsense often becomes the point
- Typically finds meaning in meaninglessness
- Usually has internal consistency to its inconsistency, rather than being random

**Emotional register:**
- Relief often comes through recognition
- Joy frequently appears in surprise
- Catharsis typically emerges through transgression
- Connection often flows through shared absurdity

## Extraction Questions

Weave these into the core phases as appropriate.

### World-Bones Additions

**The Comedy Engine:**
- "What's broken in this world that makes it funny?"
- "Are characters aware life is absurd, or obliviously sincere?"
- "What systems malfunction reliably? (bureaucracy, dating, technology, family)"
- "Is the universe conspiring against the characters, or are they their own worst enemies?"

**Tone Calibration:**
- "How dark can it go? Where's the line?"
- "Is violence slapstick or consequential?"
- "Does death exist? Is it funny, tragic, or both?"
- "What's off-limits? (Comedy with no limits often has no craft)"

**Reality Rules:**
- "How elastic is physical reality? Can characters survive impossible things?"
- "Do characters learn, or reset each scene?"
- "Is there dramatic irony? Do we know things characters don't?"

### Author Comedy Profile

**Personal Funny:**
- "What makes YOU laugh? (Not what should be funny—what actually gets you)"
- "Which comedy do you rewatch? Quote? Think about randomly?"
- "What's your embarrassing comedy preference you'd never admit?"

**Comedic Targets:**
- "What deserves mockery in your story's world?"
- "What do you find absurd about human behavior?"
- "What pretensions need puncturing?"

**Boundaries:**
- "What's never funny to you? (Honor this—forced humor fails)"
- "Where does your comedy turn mean? How do you pull back?"
- "What will your audience forgive? What won't they?"

### Character Additions

**Comedy Function:**
- "Is this character the source of comedy or the straight man reacting to it?"
- "What's their fatal flaw that generates humor?"
- "What do they take seriously that they shouldn't? Vice versa?"
- "What's the gap between how they see themselves and reality?"

**Comic Vulnerability:**
- "What embarrasses them? What would mortify them?"
- "What's the thing they'd never admit wanting?"
- "Where does their dignity crack?"
- "What's the lie they tell themselves?"

## Trope Catalog

Tropes are tools, not sins. Know which you're invoking and which you're subverting.

### Comedic Archetypes

**The Straight Man**
- Anchor of sanity in chaos
- Their exasperation IS the comedy
- Danger: becomes bland, reactive, joyless
- Subversion: reveal their hidden absurdity; they're stranger than they pretend

**The Fool**
- Bumbles through, accidentally succeeds or fails spectacularly
- Wisdom in idiocy, or just idiocy
- Danger: too stupid to care about; crosses into cruelty
- Subversion: the fool knows exactly what they're doing

**The Trickster**
- Chaos agent, rule-breaker, pot-stirrer
- Enjoys the game more than the goal
- Danger: becomes unlikeable, cruel, or exhausting
- Subversion: the trickster craves order they'd never admit wanting

**The Pompous Ass**
- Overinflated dignity, begging for deflation
- Authority unearned, confidence unwarranted
- Danger: too easy a target; becomes one-note punching bag
- Subversion: give them a moment of genuine competence or vulnerability

**The Lovable Disaster**
- Can't get out of their own way
- Good intentions, catastrophic execution
- Danger: why does anyone tolerate them?
- Subversion: their disaster creates accidental value

**The Deadpan**
- Unreactive to absurdity, or reacts to wrong things
- Dry observation as commentary
- Danger: becomes monotonous, emotionally absent
- Subversion: explosive break from deadpan reveals depths

**The Chaos Goblin**
- Delights in disorder
- Escalates for fun
- Danger: annoying, consequence-free agent of plot
- Subversion: faces consequences they didn't expect; chaos comes home

### Comedy Types

**Situational Comedy**
- Humor from circumstances, misunderstandings, clashing contexts
- "Fish out of water" structures
- The wedding where everything goes wrong
- Requires clear setup of normal before abnormal invasion

**Character Comedy**
- Humor from personality, perspective, worldview
- Characters are funny; situations reveal the funny
- Monologue, observation, reaction
- Requires distinctive voice, clear comic lens

**Verbal Comedy**
- Wit, wordplay, banter, timing
- The right wrong word
- Rhythm as punchline
- Requires ear for dialogue, ruthless editing

**Physical Comedy**
- Bodies in space, failure of grace
- Slapstick, pratfalls, bits
- The dignity of seriousness plus indignity of physics
- Requires visual thinking even in prose

### Structural Tropes

**The Running Gag**
- Repeated element that gains power through repetition
- Must escalate or transform to stay funny
- Third appearance usually pays off
- Danger: beating dead horses

**The Callback**
- Earlier element returns in new context
- Reward for audience attention
- Creates architecture of comedy
- Works best when callback is inevitable but surprising

**The Brick Joke**
- Setup so early it's forgotten, payoff when least expected
- Ultimate delayed gratification
- Requires long-form thinking
- One of comedy's most satisfying structures

**The Escalation**
- Situation worsens systematically
- Each attempt to fix makes worse
- Comedy of compounding errors
- Must have logic—one thing leads to next

**The Misdirect**
- Set up expectation, deliver different
- Subverted expectations as comedy engine
- Works once per pattern; don't repeat the same trick

**The Cutaway/Flashback**
- Interrupt narrative for comedic tangent
- Timing is everything—know when to cut and when to return
- Can become crutch; use deliberately

## Style and Voice

### Prose Texture

Comedy prose tends toward:
- Precision (the exact right word, no approximations)
- Rhythm (setups need runway; punchlines need punch)
- Brevity at impact points (don't step on the laugh)
- Breath control (know where the laugh goes; don't write through it)
- Strategic digression (the tangent that illuminates)

**The Comedic Sentence:**
- Setup creates expectation
- Middle sustains or misdirects
- End lands the joke (put the funny word last)
- "I've had a perfectly wonderful evening, but this wasn't it." — the unexpected lands last

**Avoid:**
- Signaling jokes ("Hilariously," "Comically," "Funnily enough")
- Explaining after the punchline (let it breathe)
- Trying too hard (desperation smells)
- Joke density that exhausts (contrast matters)

### Dialogue Considerations

**Banter requires:**
- Equal footing (unbalanced wit isn't banter, it's bullying)
- Genuine exchange (each line builds on last)
- Subtext (they're saying one thing, meaning another)
- Chemistry (you should want more)

**Comic timing in dialogue:**
- Short lines hit harder
- Interruptions can be gold
- The pause before the response
- The non-response that says everything

**Character voice in comedy:**
- Distinctive rhythm per character
- Vocabulary as comedy (what words they'd never use, what they overuse)
- The straight man's sigh vs. the fool's enthusiasm
- How they describe the same event differently

### Comedic Voice Spectrum

**Dry/Deadpan:**
- Understatement as technique
- Emotional disconnect between content and delivery
- "The building was on fire, which seemed suboptimal"

**Manic/Energetic:**
- Overflow of enthusiasm, speed, escalation
- Stream of consciousness, tangents, returns
- Voice that can't contain itself

**Sardonic/Sarcastic:**
- Saying opposite of truth
- Eye-roll audible in prose
- Requires target and point of view

**Absurdist:**
- Logic applied to illogical premises
- Matter-of-fact about impossible things
- "The penguin, naturally, was running the meeting"

## Pacing Expectations

### Comedy Architecture

Comedy often follows:
1. **Establish Normal** — what's the baseline before chaos?
2. **Introduce Disruption** — the wrong element in the right place
3. **Escalating Attempts** — try to fix, make worse
4. **Point of No Return** — maximum chaos, collision of all elements
5. **Resolution/Reversal** — everything collapses or miraculously resolves

### The Setup-Punchline Rhythm

**Micro-level (single joke):**
- Setup: minimum necessary context
- Misdirect: optional false trail
- Punchline: unexpected but inevitable landing
- Beat: space for the laugh to land

**Macro-level (scene/chapter):**
- Multiple joke rhythms build larger patterns
- Straight sections give comedy room to pop
- The serious moment that makes the joke land harder

### Timing Principles

**The Rule of Threes:**
- Two establishes pattern, third breaks it
- Or: two normal, third escalates
- Or: two build, third subverts
- The third is where the funny lives

**Comic Tension:**
- Delay can increase payoff
- Audience anticipation as asset
- They know what's coming; the joy is watching it arrive

**The Reset:**
- After maximum chaos, return to calm
- Brief breather before next escalation
- Characters recover so they can fail again

**Pacing Dangers:**
- Too fast: exhausting, jokes blur
- Too slow: jokes die waiting
- Unrelieved comedy: becomes numb
- Every joke the same volume: monotonous

## Comedy Mechanics

### Core Theories (Applied)

**Incongruity Theory:**
- Comedy = mismatch between expectation and reality
- The surprise of the wrong thing in the right place
- Application: identify expectations, break them precisely

**Superiority Theory:**
- Laughter at others' misfortune or foolishness
- We feel momentarily elevated
- Application: make the target deserve it, or make it absurd enough to be safe

**Relief Theory:**
- Laughter releases tension
- Built-up anxiety finds escape valve
- Application: build tension, release through comedy

**Benign Violation:**
- Something wrong/threatening that's also safe/okay
- Violation without real harm
- Application: find the threatening, make it safe

### Practical Mechanics

**Bathos:**
- Sudden drop from sublime to ridiculous
- Deflating pomposity, undercutting drama
- "She gazed upon the ancient temple, thousands of years of history... and really had to pee"

**Absurdity Calibration:**
- How far from reality can you push?
- Internal logic matters even in nonsense
- Establish your absurdity ceiling early; stay consistent

**The Comedian's Rhythm:**
- Punch UP the word that matters
- Pause BEFORE the reveal
- Speed through SETUP, slow into PUNCHLINE
- End sentences on STRONG words

**Economy:**
- Cut every word that doesn't serve the joke
- Setup should be minimal
- Punchline should be tight
- "I'm sorry, I don't have time to write you a short joke"

## Common Pitfalls

**Trying Too Hard**
- Problem: desperation reads; forced humor feels worse than no humor
- Fix: trust the setup, commit fully, let silence exist

**Explaining the Joke**
- Problem: "Get it? Because he's a—" kills the laugh
- Fix: land it and move on; either they got it or they didn't

**Mean-Spirited Humor**
- Problem: punching down, cruelty without craft
- Fix: punch up or sideways; targets should be able to take it

**Character Consistency Sacrifice**
- Problem: character does something funny but wrong for them
- Fix: funny must emerge from character, not contradict it

**Joke Density Exhaustion**
- Problem: relentless comedy numbs the audience
- Fix: contrast matters; let scenes breathe

**The Wacky Vacuum**
- Problem: everything's crazy so nothing's crazy
- Fix: ground something; absurdity needs normal to push against

**Stakes-Free Farce**
- Problem: nothing matters so why should we care?
- Fix: characters must want something genuinely, even in comedy

**Comedy Without Point of View**
- Problem: just random funny things happening
- Fix: comedy should reveal something about characters, world, or truth

**The Laugh Track Trap**
- Problem: pausing for laughs that aren't earned
- Fix: trust your landing; don't signal where to laugh

## Voice Modifiers for Comedy

When extracting character voice (Phase 6b), add these dimensions for comedic characters:

```yaml
voice:
  # Standard fields plus:

  comedy_function:
    role: "straight man / fool / trickster / deadpan / etc."
    targets: [what they find absurd, mock, or misunderstand]
    blind_spots: [what they can't see about themselves]

  comedic_rhythm:
    tempo: "rapid-fire / deliberate / variable"
    style: "verbal / physical / situational / reactive"
    signature_pattern: [catchphrases, recurring bits, verbal tics]

  dignity_vulnerabilities:
    embarrassment_trigger: "what humiliates them"
    pretension: "what they pretend to be"
    crack_point: "where the facade breaks"

  straight_man_dynamics:
    who_grounds_them: "if chaos agent, who reacts"
    who_they_ground: "if straight man, who they anchor"
```

## Example Seeds

Seeds that work well in comedy:

- "The one thing they lied about on their resume"
- "A talent that's impressive and entirely useless"
- "The pet that's smarter than its owner"
- "A family tradition no one remembers the origin of"
- "The emergency plan that's never actually been tested"
- "What they're definitely not overcompensating for"
- "The secret everyone knows but no one mentions"
- "A rivalry over something incredibly petty"
- "The thing they practiced saying in the mirror"
- "A backup plan that's worse than the problem"
- "The moment they peaked, according to them vs. reality"
- "What they think their catchphrase is vs. what it actually is"
- "The object that definitely has sentimental value, definitely"
- "A rule that exists because of them specifically"
- "The story they tell at parties vs. what actually happened"

## Integration Notes

**Combine with other modules:**
- **+ Romance:** comedic obstacles, banter as flirtation, the earnest moment that lands because everything else was funny
- **+ Horror:** nervous laughter, jokes that die in silence, comedy as coping mechanism
- **+ Dark Fantasy:** absurdity of immortality, monsters with embarrassing problems, fish-out-of-water with fangs
- **+ Mystery:** the Watson who asks dumb questions, the detective's ridiculous methods that work, comedic red herrings
- **+ Thriller:** tension release through humor, the quip under pressure, stakes that make the funny funnier

**Extract to:**
- `setting.yaml` → Add reality-elasticity rules, tone boundaries, comedic systems
- `entities.yaml` → Add `comedy_function`, `comedic_rhythm`, `dignity_vulnerabilities` to voice
- `arc.yaml` → Include escalation beats, callback plants, comedic reversal moments in seeds

**Tonal Integration Warning:**
Comedy mixed with other genres requires careful calibration. The comedy should enhance, not undercut. Serious moments must be protected from the comedic voice—or deliberately invaded by it. Know which you're doing.
