## Genre Identity

These are conventions, not commandments. Know the expectations so you can meet or subvert them intentionally.

High fantasy typically unfolds in secondary worlds where magic shapes events and stakes are civilizational. Readers may expect threats beyond the personal—cosmic in scope. The narrative often features ordinary people becoming extraordinary to preserve what matters, though you can interrogate or invert these elements.

**Core tensions:**
- Individual destiny vs. free will
- Power as corruption vs. power as responsibility
- Ancient wisdom vs. necessary change
- Unity across difference vs. cultural identity
- Hope against overwhelming odds

**Emotional register:**
- Wonder more than fear
- Courage more than cleverness
- Sacrifice as highest virtue
- Grief that transforms into resolve
- Joy earned through suffering

**Defining boundaries:**
- Traditional high fantasy often features secondary worlds with their own cosmology, rather than Earth
- Readers may expect magic to exist and matter meaningfully (not merely superstition or decoration)
- High fantasy stakes often exceed personal concerns—kingdoms, worlds, ages at risk—though you can work at different scales
- Moral dimension often present (though complexity and ambiguity are welcome)

## Extraction Questions

Weave these into the core phases as appropriate.

### World-Bones Additions

**Cosmology and Creation:**
- "How was this world made? By gods? From void? Through song or war?"
- "Are the creators still present? Active? Sleeping? Dead?"
- "What cosmic forces shape reality? Light/Dark? Order/Chaos? Elements?"
- "What comes after death? Is it known or believed? What proof exists?"

**The Shape of Magic:**
- "Where does magic come from? Gods? The land? Bloodlines? Study?"
- "Who can use it? Is it rare gift or learnable skill?"
- "What does magic cost? Always? Sometimes? For forbidden workings?"
- "What can magic absolutely NOT do? What limits define its nature?"
- "Are there different traditions? Do they conflict or complement?"

**Peoples and Powers:**
- "What races or peoples exist beyond humans? How did they originate?"
- "How do different peoples understand the world differently?"
- "What ancient grudges or alliances shape current politics?"
- "Are any peoples fading? Rising? Transformed?"
- "What happened to the elder races, the first peoples, the old gods?"

**The Age Before:**
- "What was the world like before the current age? Better? Worse? Just different?"
- "What cataclysm or transformation divided then from now?"
- "What survives from the elder days? Ruins? Artifacts? Bloodlines? Curses?"
- "What knowledge was lost? What might be recovered?"

**The Current Darkness:**
- "What threatens the world? Has it threatened before?"
- "Is the enemy a being, a force, a corruption, or an inevitability?"
- "Why now? What awakened it, empowered it, or removed what held it back?"
- "Can it be destroyed? Contained? Redeemed? Only survived?"

### Character Additions

**For Chosen Ones:**
- "What chose them? Prophecy? Bloodline? Accident? Their own actions?"
- "Do they believe in their destiny? Embrace it? Rage against it?"
- "What would they be if not chosen? What do they miss?"
- "What does the choosing cost them that others don't see?"
- "Can they fail? Has any chosen one failed before?"

**For Mentors and Guides:**
- "What do they know that they're not telling? Why?"
- "What did they fail at in their own time?"
- "What do they see in the protagonist they don't see in themselves?"
- "When will they step aside? When must they be lost?"

**For Companions:**
- "What does each bring that others cannot?"
- "What would make them leave? What keeps them when leaving makes sense?"
- "How does the group become more than its members?"
- "Who is the heart? The conscience? The strength? The laughter?"

**For Antagonists:**
- "What do they believe they're accomplishing? Are they wrong?"
- "Were they ever different? Could they be again?"
- "What's their legitimate grievance, if any?"
- "Do they know they're the villain? Would they care?"

### Quest Architecture

- "What must be found, destroyed, protected, or reached?"
- "Why can't someone more qualified do this?"
- "What happens if they fail? How final is failure?"
- "What must they learn along the way? Skills? Truths? Themselves?"
- "How does completing the quest change them? The world?"

## Trope Catalog

Tropes are tools, not sins. Know which you're invoking and which you're subverting.

### Protagonist Archetypes

**The Chosen One**
- Destiny marked by prophecy, bloodline, or circumstance
- The world narrows to them; their choices ripple outward
- Danger: removes agency, makes protagonist passive recipient of plot
- Danger: others exist only to serve their destiny
- Subversion: choice matters more than chosenness—anyone could be chosen, they chose to act
- Subversion: the prophecy is misinterpreted, manipulated, or self-fulfilling

**The Reluctant Hero**
- Didn't want this, wants to go home, keeps getting pulled back
- Audience surrogate who must grow into the role
- Danger: whining without growth, refusal long past believable
- Danger: humility that's actually false modesty
- Subversion: their reluctance is wisdom—they know what this will cost
- Subversion: they never stop being reluctant, but act anyway

**The Rightful King**
- Heir in exile, bloodline concealed, true claim to power
- Political legitimacy meets personal worthiness
- Danger: bloodline as merit, inherited superiority
- Danger: "good ruler" never examined, power assumed benevolent
- Subversion: blood means nothing without earned trust
- Subversion: claiming the throne is the beginning of hard work, not the reward

**The Last of Their Kind**
- Bearer of dying culture, language, magic, or bloodline
- Walking elegy, living memorial
- Danger: tragic backstory as only personality trait
- Subversion: they're not preserving—they're transforming, choosing what survives

### Support Structures

**The Fellowship/Party**
- Diverse group united by shared purpose
- Each member essential, irreplaceable
- Danger: token diversity, each race gets one representative
- Danger: fellowship exists to orbit protagonist
- Subversion: the group disagrees, fractures, reforms stronger
- Subversion: there is no leader—or everyone takes turns

**The Wise Mentor**
- Gandalf, Obi-Wan, Dumbledore—power held back, wisdom dispensed
- Must be lost for protagonist to fully rise
- Danger: deus ex machina who solves everything until conveniently absent
- Danger: cryptic for no reason except plot convenience
- Subversion: the mentor is wrong about important things
- Subversion: the mentor's generation caused this problem

**The Loyal Companion**
- Samwise, Horatio, the one who stays
- Courage that isn't flashy but is essential
- Danger: flattened to sidekick, servant, comic relief
- Subversion: they're the real hero—just not the famous one
- Subversion: their loyalty is tested, and they fail, and they come back

### Antagonist Archetypes

**The Dark Lord**
- Ultimate evil, cosmic threat, power beyond mortal scale
- Often absent/distant, represented by lieutenants
- Danger: so abstract they lack personality; evil for evil's sake
- Danger: backstory that tries to make them sympathetic and just makes them pathetic
- Subversion: they were the hero of another age
- Subversion: they're not evil—they're an embodiment of natural force (entropy, change, death)

**The Fallen One**
- Once good, now corrupted—angel become demon, hero become tyrant
- Living warning of what protagonist could become
- Danger: fall is one tragic moment, not a process of choices
- Subversion: the fall was actually a rise from their perspective
- Subversion: they can still be reached—but at what cost?

**The Servant of Darkness**
- Lieutenant, corrupted wizard, ambitious general
- Often more present threat than distant dark lord
- Danger: generic evil minion
- Subversion: they're trying to destroy the dark lord from within
- Subversion: they became this to save something they loved

### Structural Tropes

**The Prophecy**
- Ancient words that shape current events
- Creates narrative weight, sense of destiny
- Danger: removes suspense (we know how it ends)
- Danger: characters become puppets of predetermined outcome
- Subversion: prophecy has multiple valid interpretations
- Subversion: the prophecy was always metaphor, mistaken for literal

**The Ancient Evil Awakens**
- What was bound breaks free; what was defeated returns
- Explains why now, creates urgency
- Danger: cyclical evil cheapens stakes (they'll just beat it again)
- Subversion: this time is different—the world has changed
- Subversion: it never actually slept; we just stopped looking

**The Quest Object**
- Ring, sword, gem, book—artifact that matters
- Focal point for journey, tangible goal
- Danger: object becomes only point; world just backdrop to fetch quest
- Subversion: the object is decoy, symbol, or less important than journey
- Subversion: destroying it is only the beginning

**The Lost Knowledge**
- Ancient libraries, forgotten languages, sealed tombs
- The past holds keys to current survival
- Danger: convenient discovery of exactly what's needed
- Subversion: the knowledge was suppressed for good reason
- Subversion: understanding it requires becoming something else

## Style and Voice

### Prose Texture

High fantasy prose tends toward:
- Elevated register without becoming archaic
- Rich description of landscape, architecture, craft
- Weight given to names, titles, formal speech
- Seasonal and astronomical awareness (the world has rhythms)
- History present in the now (ruins, old roads, ancestral weapons)

**Calibrate for:**
- Wonder: the world should feel vast, ancient, marvelous
- Weight: actions matter; words spoken cannot be unspoken
- Clarity in action: epic battles need choreography, not blur
- Interiority in stillness: the quiet moments reveal character

**Avoid:**
- Faux-medieval that's just modern speech with "thee" inserted
- Travelogue description that forgets the travelers
- Battle scenes that are all noise and no stakes
- Treating worldbuilding as substitute for character

### Dialogue Considerations

**Ancient beings might:**
- Speak with precision that sounds formal to mortal ears
- Reference ages, events, beings unknown to listener
- Show patience or impatience with mortal timeframes
- Remember when words meant different things

**Common folk might:**
- Use dialect, regional expressions, occupational jargon
- Know lore in practical form (weather signs, old warnings)
- Distrust grand pronouncements from fancy strangers
- Possess wisdom that scholars lack

**Nobles and rulers might:**
- Speak in we/our when representing their people
- Use courtesy as armor and weapon
- Never forget they're performing for invisible audiences
- Show different registers for council and confidence

**Wizards and scholars might:**
- Precise terminology, technical vocabulary
- Frustration when others don't understand distinctions
- Quotes and references others don't recognize
- The habit of explaining, even when not asked

### Naming Conventions

Names carry weight in high fantasy. Consider:

**Personal names:**
- Linguistic consistency within cultures
- Meaning that may or may not be known to bearers
- Use-names vs. true names vs. titles earned
- How names change (coming of age, great deeds, falls from grace)

**Place names:**
- Etymology that reflects history (who named it, when, why)
- Common names vs. old names vs. true names
- Translation between cultures
- What the land was called before current inhabitants

**Magic and power:**
- Named spells, techniques, traditions
- Artifacts with histories embedded in names
- Words of power, true speech, the language of creation

## Pacing Expectations

### Quest Structure

High fantasy often follows the monomyth, but with variations:

1. **The Ordinary World** — life before the call (brief; story begins when it ends)
2. **The Call and Crossing** — drawn into larger conflict, leaving home
3. **Gathering** — allies found, fellowship formed, purpose clarified
4. **The Road of Trials** — challenges that test and teach
5. **The Innermost Cave** — approach to greatest danger
6. **The Ordeal** — death and rebirth (literal or metaphorical)
7. **The Reward** — what is gained, at what cost
8. **The Return** — bringing change back to the world

### Scope Escalation

Stakes typically rise in waves:
- Personal → Local → Regional → National → Cosmic
- Each victory reveals larger threat
- Heroes grow to meet growing challenge
- But retain personal stakes even at cosmic scale

### Battle and Climax

Epic confrontations require:
- Clear stakes (what happens if they lose)
- Individual moments within mass action
- Cost—victory without sacrifice feels hollow
- The small action that turns the tide
- Aftermath that honors what was lost

### Breathing Room

Even epic narratives need:
- Moments of beauty, peace, laughter
- Relationships deepened in quiet
- The Shire to miss, the home to remember
- Earned rest before earned trials

## Common Pitfalls

**The Tolkien Clone**
- Problem: middle-earth with serial numbers filed off—elves, dwarves, orcs
- Fix: build from YOUR mythology, culture, obsessions; ask what only you would create

**The Infodump Swamp**
- Problem: three-page histories interrupting present action
- Fix: worldbuilding revealed through character experience and necessity

**Flat Evil**
- Problem: antagonists who are evil because evil, want destruction for destruction
- Fix: coherent philosophy, even if repugnant; they believe they're right

**The Tourist Protagonist**
- Problem: farm boy just passes through interesting places without changing them
- Fix: choices have consequences; the world responds to protagonist

**Prophecy as Spoiler**
- Problem: we know how it ends, just watching checkboxes fill
- Fix: prophecy that can be interpreted multiple ways; fulfillment that surprises

**Chosen One Passivity**
- Problem: protagonist's main trait is being chosen; things happen TO them
- Fix: chosenness creates opportunity; character creates outcome

**Medieval Europe Plus Magic**
- Problem: uncritical pseudo-medieval setting, monarchism assumed good
- Fix: interrogate the politics; what does just rule actually mean?

**The Competence Vacuum**
- Problem: protagonist is special because everyone else is inexplicably useless
- Fix: others are capable; protagonist's role is specific, not universal

**Scope Creep**
- Problem: world-saving stakes from page one, nowhere to escalate
- Fix: start personal, let cosmic stakes emerge

## Magic System Design

### The Hard/Soft Spectrum

**Hard Magic:**
- Clear rules, consistent limitations, costs
- Reader can predict what's possible
- Problem-solving feels earned
- Risk: becomes technology with funny names

**Soft Magic:**
- Mysterious, numinous, wondrous
- Used sparingly, often by non-protagonists
- Creates wonder and danger
- Risk: deus ex machina, arbitrary solutions

**Hybrid Approach:**
- Protagonist's magic has clear rules
- Larger cosmic magic remains mysterious
- Reader understands what protagonist can do
- World retains wonder beyond protagonist's ken

### Essential Questions

**Source:**
- Where does power come from? (gods, world, self, study, bloodline)
- Can it run out? For individual? For world?

**Access:**
- Who can use it? (everyone, few, one tradition, multiple paths)
- Can ability be gained? Lost? Stolen? Taught?

**Cost:**
- What does magic take? (energy, years, sanity, soul, sacrifice)
- Is cost immediate or deferred?
- What happens when cost isn't paid?

**Limits:**
- What is impossible? (death reversal, true creation, certain knowledge)
- Why are these limits? (law of nature, god's will, fundamental balance)
- What happens when limits are transgressed?

### Magic and Society

Consider how magic shapes:
- Power structures (who rules, why)
- Economy (what's valuable, what's common)
- Warfare (how battles are fought)
- Daily life (what's easy that shouldn't be)
- Religion (is magic divine, separate, heretical)
- Social structure (mages as class, caste, guild)

## Example Seeds

Seeds that work well in high fantasy:

**Cosmological:**
- "The god that died to make this world"
- "What the stars are, really"
- "The language that was spoken before language"
- "Where magic goes when it's used"
- "What lives in the space between worlds"

**Historical:**
- "The kingdom that was before the kingdoms"
- "Why no one goes to that mountain anymore"
- "The war that broke the elder races"
- "A crown that has never had a peaceful succession"
- "The library that burned, and what survived"

**Personal:**
- "The mentor's greatest mistake"
- "What the chosen one dreams about home"
- "The antagonist when they were young and hopeful"
- "A companion's secret that would shatter the fellowship"
- "The moment they knew they couldn't go back"

**Object and Place:**
- "A sword that remembers every battle"
- "The door in the palace no one opens"
- "A map that shows what was, not what is"
- "The tree that marks the center of the world"
- "An artifact that doesn't want to be found"

**Conflict:**
- "The enemy's one legitimate grievance"
- "What the dark lord thinks they're saving"
- "Two prophecies that can't both be true"
- "The ally who will have to be fought"
- "Victory that would cost too much"

## Character Depth Extensions

Genre-specific entity fields for character files. The calibrator adds these sections based on active genre modules.

### Extraction Questions

**Destiny Relationship:**
- "How do they relate to prophecy or fate—do they resist, embrace, deny its existence?"
- "If they're 'chosen': do they want this? Did they ask for it? What did prophecy cost them?"
- "If they're NOT chosen: how do they feel about the one who is? Resentment? Relief? Protectiveness?"
- "What do they think about free will vs. fate? Has experience taught them anything about that?"

**Magical Identity:**
- "What's their connection to magic? Born with it? Learned? Stolen? Refused?"
- "How does magic manifest for them specifically? What does it look/feel/cost?"
- "What's their relationship to magical power—fear, wonder, tool, curse, birthright?"
- "What does using magic cost them? What happens when they overextend?"

**Cultural Roots:**
- "What race, kingdom, faction, culture shaped them? How does it show?"
- "What traditions do they maintain? Which have they rejected? Why?"
- "How do they relate to their people's history—proud inheritor, shameful descendant, indifferent individual?"
- "What cultural assumptions do they carry that they don't even see as assumptions?"

**Honor Architecture:**
- "What oaths have they taken? To whom? What do those oaths demand?"
- "What code do they follow—knightly? Religious? Personal? Tribal?"
- "What would make them break their word? What's unbreakable no matter what?"
- "When does honor conflict with survival? With love? With the greater good? What do they choose?"

**Quest Psychology:**
- "What drives them forward when hope fails? Duty? Stubbornness? Love? Spite?"
- "What memory do they carry that sustains them in the dark?"
- "What would make them abandon the quest? What line would have to be crossed?"
- "Who are they doing this for—the world, themselves, someone specific, the dead?"

### Examples

**Destiny Relationship — Example:**
```yaml
destiny_relationship:
  stance: "Resist. Prophecies are excuses for powerful people to manipulate the desperate."
  chosen_burden: "Not chosen, thank the gods. Watches the chosen one crumble under weight they didn't ask for."
  relationship_to_chosen: "Protective. Angry. Someone has to keep them alive since fate clearly doesn't care about the details."
  free_will_belief: "Used to believe in pure choice. Seen too many prophecies come true. Now believes fate sets the destination but the path is theirs."
```

**Magical Identity — Example:**
```yaml
magical_identity:
  connection_source: "Born with it. Family gift going back six generations. Skipped their sibling, landed on them."
  manifestation: "Green fire that doesn't burn. Smells like forest after rain. Leaves their hands tingling for hours."
  power_relationship: "Birthright and burden. It's who they are, but it's also why they can never be normal."
  cost_of_use: "Minor spells: fatigue. Major workings: nosebleeds, tremors. Nearly died once pushing too far—won't forget that."
```

**Cultural Roots — Example:**
```yaml
cultural_roots:
  origin_culture: "Northern Clans, mountain folk. Harsh winters breed hard people."
  traditions_kept: ["Never break guest-right", "Braid a token for the dead", "First hunt at thirteen"]
  traditions_rejected: ["Blood feuds—watched them destroy their village", "Men don't heal—learned herbcraft anyway"]
  historical_relationship: "Proud of resilience. Ashamed of insularity. Wish the clans had accepted help before the famine."
  unconscious_assumptions: ["Loud voices mean honesty", "Silence means deception", "City folk are soft", "Comfort is weakness"]
```

**Honor Architecture — Example:**
```yaml
honor_architecture:
  oaths_taken:
    - "Knightly vow: protect the weak, speak truth, show mercy"
    - "Personal vow: find who killed their mentor"
  code_followed: "Knightly code learned young, but interpreted through experience—mercy doesn't mean stupidity"
  breaking_point: "Would break oath to protect innocent lives. Honor's meaningless if everyone's dead."
  the_unbreakable: "Never harm a child. Never. That's where the line is."
  honor_vs_conflict: "Honor vs. survival: honor usually wins. Honor vs. saving someone they love: love wins and they hate themselves for it."
```

**Quest Psychology — Example:**
```yaml
quest_psychology:
  driving_force: "Stubbornness and spite. The darkness said they'd fail. Determined to prove it wrong."
  sustaining_memory: "Their mentor's last words: 'Finish what I started.' Haven't failed them yet."
  abandonment_threshold: "If completing the quest meant becoming the villain—they'd walk away. Won't save the world by destroying their soul."
  who_its_for: "Started for the mentor. Continued for the world. Now? For everyone who said they couldn't."
```

## Integration Notes

**Combine with other modules:**
- **+ Romance:** personal stakes within epic scope; love that survives saving the world
- **+ Mystery:** what is the true nature of threat? who is the traitor?
- **+ Horror:** the darkness is not just enemy but existential dread; magic has terrible costs
- **+ Political:** the quest exists within power structures; alliances and betrayals
- **+ Coming of Age:** hero's journey as literal maturation; the world and self discovered together

**Tension with:**
- **Grimdark:** high fantasy tends toward hope; blend carefully or choose
- **Urban Fantasy:** different worlds, different assumptions; mashups need care
- **Hard SF:** magic systems can approach science; maintain wonder or commit to system

**Extract to:**
- `setting.yaml` → Add cosmology, magic system rules, ages of history, peoples
- `entities.yaml` → Add chosen_one_dynamics, mentor_secrets, fellowship_bonds
- `arc.yaml` → Include quest_stages, prophecy_beats, sacrifice_moments in seeds
