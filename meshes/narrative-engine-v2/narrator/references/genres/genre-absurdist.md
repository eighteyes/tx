# Genre Module: Absurdist Fiction
# Logic-defying but internally consistent, meaninglessness as meaning
# Load alongside core game-maker.md extraction

## Genre Identity

These are conventions, not commandments. Know the expectations so you can meet or subvert them intentionally.

Absurdist fiction typically operates in the gap between human need for meaning and the universe's refusal to provide it. The world often follows rules—just not ones that make sense by conventional logic. Unlike surrealism (which dreams) or nonsense (which plays), absurdism tends to confront: it places rational beings in irrational systems and watches them cope, adapt, or break.

**Core tensions:**
- Order vs. chaos (order tends to win, but the order is insane)
- Meaning-seeking vs. meaning-absent
- Logic vs. experience (logic suggests this shouldn't work; it does anyway)
- Individual agency vs. incomprehensible systems
- Communication attempted vs. communication failed

**Emotional register:**
- Deadpan more than wacky
- Unease more than confusion
- Recognition more than bewilderment ("this feels wrong but I know this feeling")
- Laughter as a common sane response to insanity
- Existential vertigo dressed in mundane clothing

**What absurdism typically distinguishes itself from:**
- **Not surrealism:** Surrealism often follows dream-logic and symbolic association; absurdism typically follows a logic, just an impossible one
- **Not nonsense:** Nonsense often delights in meaninglessness; absurdism tends to mourn it (even while laughing)
- **Not random:** Random events have no internal consistency; absurdist worlds typically maintain ironclad rules that happen to be mad
- **Not automatically comedy:** The absurd can be tragic, bleak, even horrifying—Kafka's works rarely aim for laughs

## Extraction Questions

Weave these into the core phases as appropriate.

### World-Bones Additions

**The Broken Physics:**
- "What fundamental assumption about reality is simply wrong here?"
- "What impossible thing does everyone treat as mundane?"
- "What rule governs this world that would be absurd in ours?"
- "Is the strangeness visible or has it been bureaucratized into normalcy?"

**The System:**
- "What institution, process, or structure embodies the absurdity?"
- "Who administers the incomprehensible? Do they understand it?"
- "What forms must be filled out? What permissions sought? From whom?"
- "What is the punishment for violations no one can define?"

**The Gap:**
- "Where is meaning expected? Where is it absent?"
- "What do characters believe should make sense that doesn't?"
- "What question can never be answered in this world?"
- "What obvious thing can no one see or say?"

### Character Additions

**For Absurdist Protagonists:**
- "Do they notice the absurdity or are they native to it?"
- "How do they cope: denial, acceptance, rebellion, or adaptation?"
- "What do they still pursue despite (or because of) the meaninglessness?"
- "What small dignities do they cling to?"
- "Are they Sisyphus (aware and continuing) or Josef K. (confused and compliant)?"
- "What breaks them? Or do they break at all?"

**For System Characters:**
- "Do they serve the absurdity knowingly or unknowingly?"
- "Are they bureaucrat, victim, or both?"
- "What do they believe they're accomplishing?"
- "How do they justify the unjustifiable?"

### Situation Questions

- "What is the impossible task that must be completed?"
- "What's the loop? Where does it start over?"
- "What transformation has occurred that no one can explain?"
- "What are they waiting for? Will it come?"
- "What crime has been committed (if any) and does it matter?"

## Trope Catalog

Tropes are tools, not crutches. Know what each offers and what it risks.

### Structural Absurdities

**The Bureaucratic Nightmare**
- Forms for forms. Permissions to request permissions. Offices that refer to other offices.
- Characters reduced to case numbers in systems that process nothing.
- What it offers: Satire of institutional dehumanization; the horror of being a number
- Danger: Becomes Kafka-lite, surface-level frustration without existential weight
- Subversion: The bureaucracy actually works—just not in any way that benefits humans

**The Endless Loop**
- Events repeat. Progress erases. Tomorrow is today.
- The character may or may not notice; the audience always does.
- What it offers: Sisyphean persistence, the question of whether effort matters
- Danger: Becomes tedious if the iterations lack variation
- Subversion: Breaking the loop changes nothing, or makes things worse

**The Unexplained Transformation**
- Woke up as a bug. Now have two noses. Everyone has forgotten you're human.
- The change is physical, social, or metaphysical—never explained.
- What it offers: Alienation made literal; identity stripped bare
- Danger: The metaphor overwhelms the story
- Subversion: The transformation is an improvement no one will admit

**The Sisyphean Task**
- Must complete an endless, meaningless labor. Digging holes to fill them.
- The task may be assigned or self-imposed.
- What it offers: Meaning through persistence, dignity through refusal to quit
- Danger: Reads as torture porn without the protagonist's relationship to the task
- Subversion: The task is completed—and then what?

**The Trial With No Crime**
- Accused. Guilty. Of what? Perhaps you'll be told. Perhaps not.
- The trial may be literal (courts) or metaphorical (social judgment).
- What it offers: Examination of guilt, innocence, and systems that need neither
- Danger: Protagonist is entirely passive victim
- Subversion: The protagonist is guilty—of something real but irrelevant

**Waiting for Nothing**
- Something is coming. Someone is arriving. Just wait.
- The waiting defines existence; arrival would end it.
- What it offers: The persistence of hope against evidence
- Danger: Nothing happens (fine for theater; harder in prose)
- Subversion: What they're waiting for arrives, and it changes nothing

### Character Positions

**The Unaware Native**
- Born into absurdity, notices nothing strange.
- Contrasts with protagonist or reader's perspective.
- What it offers: Normalizes the abnormal, heightens protagonist's alienation
- Danger: Becomes cartoonish "crazy person" rather than adapted human
- Subversion: Their unawareness is performance—they know but can't say

**The Futile Rebel**
- Fights the system, accomplishes nothing.
- May be heroic or delusional (often both).
- What it offers: The question of whether resistance matters without success
- Danger: Reads as mocking resistance itself
- Subversion: The rebellion accomplishes something—just nothing intended

**The Cheerful Functionary**
- Administers madness with a smile.
- Not malicious; simply doing their job.
- What it offers: The banality of absurdity
- Danger: Too easy a villain
- Subversion: They're the most trapped of all

**Those Who Cannot Escape**
- Every exit leads back. Every solution creates the problem.
- Aware of the trap, unable to leave it.
- What it offers: Claustrophobia of circumstance
- Danger: Relentless hopelessness without purpose
- Subversion: They escape—into somewhere worse

**Those Who Adapted Too Well**
- Have made peace with the absurd so completely they've become part of it.
- Former rebels, now administrators.
- What it offers: Warning and possibility in one
- Danger: Reads as "giving up is good, actually"
- Subversion: Their adaptation preserved something others lost

## Style and Voice

### Prose Texture

Absurdist prose tends toward:
- Deadpan delivery of impossible events
- Bureaucratic language in wrong contexts
- Precision describing the nonsensical
- Matter-of-fact impossibility ("The door led to last Tuesday.")
- Mundane details amid chaos (what he ate for breakfast during the apocalypse)
- Repetition as rhythm and trap

**The Deadpan Register:**
"The form required three signatures: his own, his supervisor's, and that of his grandmother, who had died in 1987. The clerk explained that dead relatives could sign via the appropriate form, which required three signatures."

**Avoid:**
- Wackiness (the absurd is not zany)
- Explanation (the moment you explain the impossible, it becomes fantasy)
- Winking at the audience (characters take their world seriously)
- Randomness pretending to be absurdity

### Dialogue Considerations

**Characters in absurdist worlds might:**
- Talk past each other with perfect confidence
- Answer questions that weren't asked
- Repeat phrases as if repetition equals meaning
- Use logic impeccably to reach impossible conclusions
- Argue passionately about irrelevant distinctions
- Fail to communicate while exchanging many words

**Circular dialogue:**
"Why am I here?"
"Because you were summoned."
"Who summoned me?"
"The summons will explain."
"Where is the summons?"
"You have it. You're here."

**Logical illogic:**
"You can't leave without the exit pass."
"Where do I get the exit pass?"
"From the office outside."
"But I can't leave to get there."
"That's why you need the exit pass."

### Tonal Range

Absurdism is not one flavor:

- **Kafkaesque:** Dread, institutional horror, bureaucratic nightmare, the individual crushed
- **Camusian:** Defiant, philosophical, finding meaning in meaning's absence
- **Ionescan:** Language failing, communication collapsing, words losing meaning
- **Beckettian:** Waiting, stasis, persistence despite nothing, dark comedy
- **Heller-esque:** War/institution as absurd system, dark humor, catch-22 logic

## Pacing Expectations

### The Anti-Climax as Feature

Traditional narrative promises resolution. Absurdism promises only continuation—or arbitrary ending.

**Escalation without resolution:**
- Things get stranger, more intense, more trapped
- The protagonist gets closer to... something
- And then: nothing, or everything, or the same thing as before

**The Sisyphean rhythm:**
1. Attempt (try to escape/solve/understand)
2. Progress (seems to be working)
3. Collapse (back to zero, or worse)
4. Repeat (with or without learning)

### Tension Architecture

1. **Intrusion** — something is wrong (or was always wrong, now noticed)
2. **Investigation** — attempt to understand the logic
3. **Adaptation** — learn the rules (they don't help)
4. **Escalation** — stakes rise, absurdity deepens
5. **Crisis** — point of maximum pressure
6. **Resolution/Non-resolution** — escape, acceptance, continuation, or death (any of which may mean victory or defeat)

### Time Distortion

Absurdist narratives often fracture time:
- Loops and repetitions
- Time that doesn't pass despite events
- Tomorrow becoming yesterday
- Characters aging while the world doesn't (or vice versa)

## Common Pitfalls

**Random Is Not Absurd**
- Problem: "Lol so random" events with no internal logic
- Fix: The absurd follows rules—impossible rules, consistently applied

**Meaninglessness Without Purpose**
- Problem: Nothing matters, therefore nothing matters to the reader
- Fix: Characters must care, even if caring is futile; readers follow investment, not meaning

**The Punchline Treadmill**
- Problem: Each scene tries to be weirder than the last; exhausting
- Fix: Absurdity is atmosphere, not escalating gags; some scenes breathe

**Kafka as Costume**
- Problem: Surface-level bureaucracy jokes without existential weight
- Fix: The absurdity must cost something; there must be stakes

**Explaining the Inexplicable**
- Problem: Revealing why the world is this way
- Fix: The question is "how do we live here," not "why is it this way"

**Protagonist as Pure Victim**
- Problem: Character has no agency, only suffering
- Fix: Even Sisyphus chooses to push; there must be will, even futile will

**Forgetting the Human**
- Problem: So focused on the absurd situation that characters become props
- Fix: The absurd is the world; the story is still about people in it

## Absurd Logic Design

The key to absurdism: internal consistency of the impossible.

### Establishing Rules

Every absurdist world needs rules. They don't need to be stated—but they must be consistent.

**Example rule types:**
- "All doors lead to the room you just left, unless you knock twice"
- "Questions are answered, but never the question you asked"
- "Forms require signatures from the dead, who can sign if you file the right form"
- "Time passes only when unobserved"
- "The punishment for unknown crimes is to discover what crime you committed"

### Testing Consistency

For each absurd element, ask:
- What happens when this rule meets normal human behavior?
- What happens when two absurd rules interact?
- Who benefits from this impossibility?
- Who suffers?
- How have people adapted?

### Layers of Absurdity

Strong absurdist worlds layer:
1. **The Big Absurdity** — the fundamental break from reality
2. **The Institutional Absurdity** — how systems have formed around the break
3. **The Social Absurdity** — how people have normalized it
4. **The Personal Absurdity** — how individuals cope

## Example Seeds

Seeds that work well in absurdist fiction:

- "The form that creates the problem it claims to solve"
- "The door to the office that processes complaints about the door"
- "What the waiting room waited for before there were people"
- "The crime you're guilty of but will never be told"
- "The transformation everyone pretends didn't happen"
- "The job you cannot quit, describe, or stop doing"
- "The meeting scheduled for a date that doesn't exist"
- "The promotion that made everything worse, which you cannot refuse"
- "What the boulder said to Sisyphus on the thousandth roll"
- "The exit that leads to the entrance"
- "The permission slip signed by your own future corpse"
- "The trial where guilt is not in question—only the crime"
- "The conversation that's been happening since before you joined it"
- "The queue that moves backward"
- "The rule everyone follows that no one can explain"

## Character Depth Extensions

Genre-specific entity fields for character files. The calibrator adds these sections based on active genre modules.

### Extraction Questions

**Reality Relationship:**
- "How do they process the absurd? Do they notice it? Accept it? Resist it?"
- "When did they realize nothing makes sense? Or have they always known?"
- "What do they cling to as 'normal' despite evidence? What illusion maintains?"
- "Are they aware they're in an absurd world, or is this just... reality to them?"

**Meaning Architecture:**
- "How do they construct meaning in meaninglessness? Ritual? Routine? Arbitrary rules? Love?"
- "What's their Sisyphean boulder—what do they keep doing despite futility?"
- "Do they believe their actions matter? Pretend they do? Stop caring?"
- "What small thing anchors them when the big things don't make sense?"

**Coping Mechanisms:**
- "How do they survive the absurd? Humor? Denial? Rage? Acceptance? Apathy?"
- "What tells—behaviors, speech patterns—reveal their coping method?"
- "When coping fails, what happens? Breakdown? Breakthrough? Different coping?"
- "What would it take for them to stop coping and start... something else?"

**The Straight Man:**
- "Are they the absurd element or the one reacting to absurdity?"
- "If reacting: what keeps them trying to make sense of it? Stubbornness? Hope? Habit?"
- "If absurd: are they aware of it? Do they do it on purpose or is this just who they are?"
- "Where's the comedy—in them or in their response to the world?"

**Existential Anchors:**
- "What small things keep them tethered when everything else is chaos?"
- "What rituals do they maintain? Why? Would they admit the ritual is meaningless?"
- "What belief do they protect even knowing it might be false?"
- "If all anchors failed simultaneously, what would they become?"

### Examples

**Reality Relationship — Example:**
```yaml
reality_relationship:
  absurdity_processing: "Notices. Can't stop noticing. Wishes they could be like the others who don't see it."
  realization_moment: "The day the forms changed but the process didn't. Realized the process was always meaningless. Just stopped pretending."
  maintained_illusions: "That someone, somewhere knows what's happening. That the system has a point. Getting harder to believe."
  awareness_level: "Painfully aware it's absurd. Everyone else acts like it's normal. The dissonance is maddening."
```

**Meaning Architecture — Example:**
```yaml
meaning_architecture:
  construction_method: "Ritual. Meticulous, pointless ritual. File everything alphabetically. Makes no difference. Do it anyway."
  sisyphean_boulder: "Stamping forms that will never be read. 20 years of stamping. Another 20 to go. The rhythm is the meaning."
  action_belief: "Don't believe it matters. Do it because not doing it would mean admitting the void. Keep stamping."
  small_anchor: "The pen. It's just a pen. But it's MY pen. In a world of nothing, at least I have the pen."
```

**Coping Mechanisms — Example:**
```yaml
coping_mechanisms:
  primary_method: "Humor. Laugh at it. If you're not laughing, you're screaming. Choose laughing."
  behavioral_tells: ["Laughs at wrong moments", "Makes jokes during serious situations", "Smiles when should be concerned"]
  failure_response: "Laughter stops. Silence. Then something breaks—could be them, could be the world. Don't know which yet."
  breaking_point: "If the humor fails AND the routine fails simultaneously. Then there's nothing left. That's the fear."
```

**The Straight Man — Example:**
```yaml
the_straight_man:
  role: "Reactor. The only one who sees how insane this all is. Everyone else is fine with it."
  if_reactor_why_persist: "Stubbornness. Refuse to accept this is normal. Someone has to keep pointing out the emperor's naked."
  if_absurd_awareness: "N/A—they're the reactor"
  comedy_location: "In their exasperation. They're right. It IS absurd. That doesn't change anything. That's the joke."
```

**Existential Anchors — Example:**
```yaml
existential_anchors:
  small_tethers: ["Morning coffee routine", "The plant on the desk", "Sorting paperwork left-to-right", "Lunch at exactly noon"]
  maintained_rituals: ["Alphabetizes everything. Files, desk items, pantry. If something's in order, something makes sense."]
  protected_belief: "That meaning exists somewhere, even if not here. Protect that belief like a candle in a storm."
  total_failure_state: "If all anchors failed: probably join the others. Stop noticing. Be native to the absurd. That terrifies them more than the void."
```

## Integration Notes

**Combine with other modules:**
- **+ Comedy:** Lean into dark humor, the laugh of recognition and horror
- **+ Horror:** The absurd as cosmic indifference; bureaucracy as eldritch
- **+ Literary Fiction:** Philosophical weight, Camus-style meditation
- **+ Science Fiction:** Absurd futures, bureaucracies of the stars
- **+ Noir:** The investigation that cannot be solved; guilt without crime

**Tonal Cautions:**
- **+ Romance:** Difficult mix; love requires meaning. Subvert: love as the one meaning in meaninglessness
- **+ Fantasy:** Magic needs rules; absurdism breaks rules. Subvert: magic bureaucracy, wizard DMV
- **+ Adventure:** Action requires stakes; absurdism undermines stakes. Subvert: the quest that accomplishes nothing gloriously

**Extract to:**
- `setting.yaml` → Add absurd rules, institutional structures, loop mechanics
- `entities.yaml` → Add `absurdity_awareness` (notices/native/adapted), `coping_mechanism`, `role_in_system`
- `arc.yaml` → Include non-resolution options, loop checkpoints, transformation beats without explanation

## The Absurdist Question

Every absurdist story ultimately asks one question:

*Given that nothing makes sense and nothing will ever make sense—now what?*

The answer is the character. The answer is the story.

Some roll the boulder again. Some sit down and refuse. Some build a home on the slope. Some forget there was ever a summit.

None of them are wrong. All of them are human.
