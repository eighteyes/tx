## Genre Identity

These are conventions, not commandments. Know the expectations so you can meet or subvert them intentionally.

Dark fantasy typically lives in the space between horror and romance, between revulsion and desire. The monster is often not defeated—it may be understood, desired, or become the protagonist. Darkness is typically aesthetic, not merely obstacle.

**Core tensions:**
- Humanity vs. monstrosity (and which is truly monstrous?)
- Desire vs. danger
- Immortality vs. connection
- Power vs. vulnerability
- Civilization vs. wild nature

**Emotional register:**
- Yearning more than fear
- Melancholy more than dread
- Passion with edge
- Beauty found in darkness

## Extraction Questions

Weave these into the core phases as appropriate.

### World-Bones Additions

**The Nature of Otherness:**
- "What kinds of beings exist beyond human? How did they come to be?"
- "What makes something 'monstrous' in this world? Is it form? Hunger? Power?"
- "Can humans become other? Can others become human? What's the cost?"
- "How do different kinds of beings age, die, or persist?"

**The Veil Between:**
- "How aware are humans of the other world? Do they fear, worship, or deny it?"
- "Where do humans and others intersect? Courts? Borders? Dreams?"
- "What keeps the worlds separate? What threatens that separation?"

**Hunger and Need:**
- "What do the non-human beings need to survive? Blood? Emotion? Souls?"
- "How do they reconcile predatory nature with connection?"
- "Is the hunger literal, metaphorical, or both?"

### Character Additions

**For Otherkin Characters:**
- "How old are they? What have they seen?"
- "What human experiences are forever alien to them?"
- "What do they envy about mortality? What do they pity?"
- "How does their inhuman nature manifest physically? Behaviorally?"
- "What's their relationship with their own kind?"
- "What breaks through their control? Rage? Desire? Grief?"

**For Human-Adjacent Characters:**
- "What draws them to the darkness rather than repels them?"
- "What did they lose that makes the monstrous feel like home?"
- "Are they prey, partner, or becoming something else?"

### Relationship Dynamics

- "What's the power differential? How is it navigated?"
- "What can the otherkin offer that humans cannot? And vice versa?"
- "What's the impossible thing this relationship asks for?"
- "How does immortal + mortal work? Is there a solution? Should there be?"
- "What's the claiming/bonding/mating dynamic, if any?"

## Trope Catalog

Tropes are tools, not sins. Know which you're invoking and which you're subverting.

### Classic Otherkin Archetypes

**The Ancient One**
- Centuries of existence, still surprised by one mortal
- Jaded until cracked open
- Danger: becomes patronizing "I've seen empires fall" bore
- Subversion: their age made them MORE vulnerable, not less

**The Feral Beast**
- More instinct than reason, barely contained
- Transformation as loss of control or liberation?
- Danger: becomes abuse-apologist "I couldn't help it"
- Subversion: the "beast" has clearer ethics than civilized beings

**The Reluctant Monster**
- Didn't choose this, fighting their nature
- Self-loathing as character trait
- Danger: endless whining, no agency
- Subversion: learns their nature isn't the problem—shame is

**The Seductive Predator**
- Danger IS the appeal
- Knows exactly what they're doing
- Danger: no interiority, becomes cardboard sexy villain
- Subversion: the seduction is their mask; vulnerability beneath

**The Otherworld Royalty**
- Fae court, vampire coven, demon hierarchy
- Political marriage, claimed consort, forbidden desire across ranks
- Danger: court politics overwhelm the personal story
- Subversion: the crown is the cage

### Relationship Tropes

**Beauty and the Beast**
- Gentleness tames the monster (or does it?)
- Risk: implies the "beast" needed fixing
- Subversion: the beast was always gentle; the world made them hide it

**Captive Romance**
- Taken, kept, chosen
- REQUIRES careful handling of consent and power
- Must earn the shift from captivity to choice
- The moment of "you can leave" must be real

**Fated Mates / Soul Bonds**
- Cosmic connection, recognition across lifetimes
- Risk: removes agency, "you have no choice but to love me"
- Subversion: fate brought them together; staying is still a choice

**Corruption Arc**
- Human becomes monstrous through love/proximity
- Transformation as loss or becoming?
- Key question: do they mourn their humanity or shed it gladly?

**Redemption Arc**
- Monster becomes more human through love
- Risk: implies they were "wrong" before
- Subversion: they don't become human—they become themselves

**Predator/Prey Dynamics**
- Hunger as metaphor for desire
- "I could destroy you and I choose not to"
- Power exchange explicit or implicit
- The prey who isn't afraid; the predator who's terrified

## Style and Voice

### Prose Texture

Dark fantasy prose tends toward:
- Sensory richness (texture, scent, temperature)
- Gothic vocabulary (ancient, shadow, hunger, ache)
- Longer sentences for atmosphere, shorter for intensity
- Body awareness (breath, heartbeat, the space between bodies)
- Nature as mirror (storms, decay, moonlight)

**Avoid:**
- Purple prose that loses meaning
- Clinical detachment during emotional peaks
- Modern slang that breaks atmosphere (unless deliberate contrast)

### Dialogue Considerations

**Ancient beings might:**
- Use older grammatical structures
- Speak with formal precision that cracks under emotion
- Reference things the other character can't understand
- Have tells that reveal age (forgetting current customs, anachronistic references)

**Feral beings might:**
- Speak less, mean more
- Communicate through action and body
- Words as unfamiliar tools
- Direct to the point of discomfort

**Court beings might:**
- Never say exactly what they mean
- Weaponize politeness
- Every word chosen for multiple audiences
- Silence as power

### Sensory Palette

Dark fantasy engages:
- **Touch:** cold skin, unexpected warmth, textures of scale/fur/shadow
- **Scent:** blood, old stone, forest decay, inhuman pheromones
- **Sound:** too-quiet heartbeats, growls felt more than heard, ancient languages
- **Taste:** communion of blood/essence, forbidden fruit
- **Proprioception:** presence felt before seen, the weight of immortal attention

## Pacing Expectations

### Tension Architecture

Dark fantasy romance often follows:
1. **Intrusion** — worlds collide, human enters monster territory or vice versa
2. **Resistance** — one or both fight the pull
3. **Revelation** — true nature exposed (physical, emotional, or both)
4. **Negotiation** — what are the rules of this impossible thing?
5. **Crisis** — external threat or internal breaking point
6. **Claiming** — choice made, bond acknowledged (not always happy)
7. **Integration** — new normal, transformed by connection

### Slow Burn Elements

- Extended periods of tension before physical contact
- Near-misses and interruptions
- The almost-touch that means everything
- Revelation in layers (never all at once)
- Earning vulnerability

### Catharsis Points

- The moment control breaks
- The first true name spoken
- The feeding/claiming/bonding scene
- "I could end you" → "I choose you"
- Transformation witnessed

## Common Pitfalls

**The Sanitized Monster**
- Problem: otherkin has no actual monstrous traits, just "spicy human"
- Fix: the inhuman elements should create real friction, not just aesthetic

**Abuse Framed as Romance**
- Problem: controlling/violent behavior excused by "he's a vampire"
- Fix: the character may struggle with impulses, but choices matter; harm has consequences

**The Helpless Human**
- Problem: human exists only to be protected, desired, claimed
- Fix: give them agency, skills, reasons the otherkin needs THEM

**Immortality Without Weight**
- Problem: 500-year-old being acts like moody 20-year-old
- Fix: centuries should SHOW—in references, in weariness, in alien perspective

**World Without Rules**
- Problem: magic/power works however plot demands
- Fix: establish constraints; the monster's limitations create tension

**All Courts, No Character**
- Problem: political intrigue drowns personal story
- Fix: court politics should pressure the relationship, not replace it

## Voice Modifiers for Entities

When extracting character voice (Phase 6b), add these dimensions for otherkin:

```yaml
voice:
  # Standard fields plus:

  age_markers:
    anachronisms: [references they still use from past eras]
    forgotten_customs: [social norms they fail to notice have changed]
    temporal_slips: [moments when they forget which century it is]

  inhuman_tells:
    - description of non-human speech patterns
    # Examples:
    # - "Sibilants elongate when hungry"
    # - "Speaks of self in plural when formal"
    # - "Sentences lack contractions—too ancient for linguistic shortcuts"
    # - "Growls underlaying words when possessive"

  control_breaks:
    trigger: "what causes the mask to slip"
    manifestation: "how the slip sounds"
```

Seeds that work well in dark fantasy:

- "A scar that shouldn't be possible on immortal flesh"
- "The name they were called before they became this"
- "A door in the castle that's always locked"

- "What the moon does to them"

- "A promise made when the world was younger"
- "The reason the forest is avoided"
- "Blood that tastes wrong—or impossibly right"
- "The portrait that resembles someone still living"

## Character Depth Extensions

Genre-specific entity fields for character files. The calibrator adds these sections based on active genre modules.

### Extraction Questions

**Corruption Architecture:**
- "How does darkness enter them? Through choice, curse, inheritance, or slow erosion?"
- "What would they sacrifice for power? For survival? For someone they love?"
- "What's the line they won't cross? What would make them cross it anyway?"
- "How do they justify what they've done? Or do they even bother justifying?"

**Monstrous Self:**
- "What part of themselves do they hide? What would others see if the mask slipped?"
- "What do they become under extreme pressure—desperate, dangerous, something other?"
- "What transformation do they fear? Becoming like their enemy? Becoming their true self?"
- "If they're already monstrous: what humanity remains? What do they cling to?"

**Forbidden Knowledge:**
- "What do they know that they shouldn't? What truth would others kill them for possessing?"
- "What did it cost them to learn this? Sanity? Innocence? Someone else's life?"
- "Do they wish they could unknow it? Or is the knowing the only thing that makes sense anymore?"
- "What would happen if they told the truth? Who would it destroy—themselves or others?"

**Dark Bargains:**
- "What deals have they made? With whom? For what price?"
- "What debts are outstanding? What payment comes due? When?"
- "If they could unmake the bargain, would they? What would they lose if they did?"
- "What would they trade now that they wouldn't have traded before? How has desperation changed them?"

**Power Relationship:**
- "How do they relate to dark/magical power? Fear it? Crave it? Both?"
- "What does using power cost them? Pain? Memory? Pieces of self?"
- "What's the seduction—strength, control, freedom, revenge? What do they tell themselves about why they use it?"
- "When do they reach for power? When do they resist? What's the difference?"

### Entity Schema Extensions

```yaml
corruption_architecture:
  entry_point: "how darkness entered (choice/curse/inheritance/erosion)"
  sacrifice_inventory: "what they'd give up for power, survival, love—in that order"
  the_line: "what they won't do (until they will)"
  justification: "how they explain their actions to themselves—or if they've stopped trying"

monstrous_self:
  the_hidden_part: "what they conceal from others"
  pressure_transformation: "what they become when desperate or cornered"
  feared_becoming: "the transformation that terrifies them"
  remaining_humanity: "if already monstrous—what human pieces they protect"
  # Examples:
  # the_hidden_part: "The hunger. The way blood smells like home."
  # pressure_transformation: "Cold. Calculating. The part that doesn't hesitate to kill."
  # feared_becoming: "Exactly like the one who turned them. Empty. Feeding without feeling."

forbidden_knowledge:
  what_they_know: "the truth that's dangerous to possess"
  cost_of_knowing: "what it took to learn (sanity/innocence/someone's life)"
  wish_to_unknow: "do they regret knowing, or is it the only truth they trust?"
  consequence_of_telling: "who it would destroy if they spoke it aloud"

dark_bargains:
  deals_made: [list of bargains, with whom, for what price]
  debts_outstanding: "what payment is still owed, when it comes due"
  unmake_cost: "if they broke the bargain, what would they lose?"
  desperation_shift: "what they'd trade now that they wouldn't have before"
  # Examples:
  # deals_made:
  #   - "Made a pact with the Hollow King: power for memory. Forgot their own name."
  #   - "Promised their firstborn to the forest witch for passage. Childless by choice ever since."

power_relationship:
  emotional_stance: "fear it/crave it/both—how they feel about their power"
  cost_of_use: "what it takes from them (pain/memory/self/life)"
  seduction: "what draws them to use it (strength/control/freedom/revenge)"
  threshold_pattern: "when they reach for it vs. when they resist—what's the trigger"
```

### Examples

**Corruption Architecture — Example:**
```yaml
corruption_architecture:
  entry_point: "Choice. Chose power over morality when their family was threatened. Would choose it again."
  sacrifice_inventory: "For power: their reputation. For survival: someone else's life. For love: their own soul."
  the_line: "Won't harm children. That's what they tell themselves. Haven't been tested enough yet."
  justification: "The world is cruel. The innocent suffer. Power is the only protection. (Starting to sound hollow.)"
```

**Monstrous Self — Example:**
```yaml
monstrous_self:
  the_hidden_part: "The pleasure they take in violence. The way winning a fight feels better than anything else."
  pressure_transformation: "Goes silent. Eyes go flat. The person disappears; the weapon remains."
  feared_becoming: "Their father—a man who loved hurting people and called it discipline."
  remaining_humanity: "Protects the weak. Remembers every name. Refuses to enjoy the kill no matter how good it feels."
```

**Forbidden Knowledge — Example:**
```yaml
forbidden_knowledge:
  what_they_know: "The gods are dead. The prayers are answered by things wearing their names."
  cost_of_knowing: "Saw it happen. Watched a god die screaming. Can't sleep without seeing it."
  wish_to_unknow: "No. The lie would be worse. At least the truth doesn't pretend to comfort."
  consequence_of_telling: "The church would burn them. The faithful would riot. Some truths kill civilizations."
```

**Dark Bargains — Example:**
```yaml
dark_bargains:
  deals_made:
    - "Traded ten years of life to save their sister. Nine years gone. One remains."
    - "Promised service to the Winter Court. Owe them three favors. Haven't called them in yet."
  debts_outstanding: "One year of life. Three favors to immortal fae. Both will be collected."
  unmake_cost: "Breaking the life bargain would kill them instantly. Breaking the fae debt would destroy their sister."
  desperation_shift: "Used to think their life was non-negotiable. Now it's just another currency."
```

**Power Relationship — Example:**
```yaml
power_relationship:
  emotional_stance: "Craves it and loathes the craving. Feels like an addict who knows the drug is poison."
  cost_of_use: "Every spell takes a memory. Small ones first. Starting to lose the important things."
  seduction: "Control. In a chaotic world, magic is the only thing that does what they want."
  threshold_pattern: "Uses it when others are in danger. Resists when it's just for themselves. Failing at that more often."
```
