# Genre Module: Slice of Life
# The poetry of ordinary days, community rhythms, small moments that mean everything
# Load alongside core game-maker.md extraction

## Genre Identity

These are conventions, not commandments. Know the expectations so you can meet or subvert them intentionally.

Slice of life typically finds the sacred in the mundane—not by adding drama, but by teaching the reader to see what was always there. The coffee shop often becomes character rather than backdrop. The changing seasons tend to function as plot. These stories rarely rely on explosions; instead, everything matters.

**Core tensions:**
- Stillness vs. restlessness (finding peace vs. seeking more)
- Belonging vs. independence
- Tradition vs. change
- Presence vs. nostalgia
- The known vs. the unknown newcomer

**Emotional register:**
- Gentle melancholy more than grief
- Contentment more than ecstasy
- Wistfulness more than longing
- Warmth with undertones of bittersweetness
- Joy in recognition, not revelation

**What makes mundane compelling (not boring):**
- Specificity—not "a meal" but "grandmother's twice-burnt rice with the crispy bottom everyone fights over"
- Stakes that matter to these people—the bakery's rent, the garden's first tomato, remembering someone's usual order
- Texture of lived experience—chipped mugs with history, squeaky floorboards that announce arrivals
- The reader's own memories activated—universal experiences rendered with enough detail to trigger personal resonance
- Meaning tends to emerge from accumulation rather than incident

## Extraction Questions

Weave these into the core phases as appropriate.

### World-Bones Additions

**The Shape of Place:**
- "What does this community smell like at dawn? At closing time?"
- "What sounds mark the hours here? Church bells? Train whistles? The lunch rush?"
- "What's the oldest building? The newest intrusion? How do locals feel about each?"
- "Where do people gather without planning to? Where do they go to be alone?"
- "What can you see from the window of the main gathering place?"

**The Texture of Time:**
- "How do the seasons change things here? What opens, closes, transforms?"
- "What annual events does everyone anticipate? Dread? Pretend to forget?"
- "What's the rhythm of a typical day? Week? What breaks the rhythm?"
- "What's been here 'forever'? What recently closed? What just opened?"

**Community Architecture:**
- "Who knows everyone's business? How do they know?"
- "What's the local gossip currently? What's the decade-old gossip still whispered?"
- "What divides the community? What unites them despite everything?"
- "Who's respected? Who's tolerated? Who's invisible?"
- "What do outsiders misunderstand about this place?"

### Character Additions

**For Rooted Characters:**
- "What keeps them here when they could leave?"
- "What do they love about this place that they'd never admit aloud?"
- "What daily rituals anchor them? What happens when the ritual breaks?"
- "Who would notice if they didn't show up one day?"
- "What do they know about this place that newcomers never will?"

**For Newcomer Characters:**
- "Why did they come here? What are they running toward or from?"
- "What surprised them about this place? What disappointed them?"
- "What local custom confuses them? Which one have they unconsciously adopted?"
- "How long until they stop feeling like a visitor?"
- "What do they bring that this place secretly needed?"

**For Community Fixtures:**
- "How did they become the person who [runs the shop/knows everyone/remembers everything]?"
- "What do they get from their role? What does it cost them?"
- "Who were they before they became 'the [baker/postmaster/neighbor everyone relies on]'?"
- "What would happen to the community if they left?"

### Relationship Dynamics

- "How do these people know each other? How long? Through what?"
- "What are they to each other that they've never named?"
- "What do they do together that feels like nothing and means everything?"
- "What's the thing they don't talk about but both remember?"
- "How do they show care without ever saying 'I love you'?"

## Trope Catalog

Tropes are tools, not sins. Know which you're invoking and which you're subverting.

### Place Archetypes

**The Gathering Place (Cafe, Bookshop, Bakery)**
- Where everyone eventually passes through
- The owner who remembers your order, your troubles, your grandmother
- What it offers: ready-made community hub, natural scene anchoring, excuse for character intersection
- Danger: becomes interchangeable cozy-shop aesthetic without specificity
- Subversion: the place is struggling, changing hands, threatened—what the regulars do to save it reveals who they are

**The Family Home**
- Multigenerational weight, every room holds memory
- Kitchen as heart, porch as threshold
- What it offers: inherited conflict and love, physical manifestation of history
- Danger: becomes sentimental museum piece
- Subversion: the home must be sold, renovated, or divided—what stays, what goes, what was hidden in the walls

**The Garden/Farm**
- Labor and seasons made visible
- Growth as metaphor and literal plot
- What it offers: built-in temporal structure, tangible stakes, sensory richness
- Danger: becomes pastoral escapism without sweat and failure
- Subversion: the garden isn't thriving—blight, drought, neglect. Tending it is grief work.

**The Small Town/Village**
- Everyone knows everyone (and everyone's business)
- The main street with its landmarks
- What it offers: closed social system, nowhere to hide, community as character
- Danger: becomes either idyllic fantasy or sneering satire
- Subversion: the town is dying, changing, being discovered—what does preservation cost?

### Character Archetypes

**The Keeper of the Gathering Place**
- Witnesses everyone's story, tells none
- Dispenses wisdom with the daily special
- What they offer: central node, audience surrogate, gentle guide
- Danger: becomes wisdom-dispensing vending machine without their own needs
- Subversion: they're exhausted, they want to leave, they're staying out of obligation—their care has a price

**The Regular Customers**
- Same seat, same order, same time
- Their presence is the clock, their absence is alarm
- What they offer: texture, continuity, micro-stakes
- Danger: become furniture, interchangeable quirk-delivery systems
- Subversion: the regular who stops coming—why? The regular who changes their order—what shifted?

**The Newcomer**
- Fresh eyes on familiar things
- Excuse for exposition that feels natural
- What they offer: catalyst for change, outside perspective, questions the locals stopped asking
- Danger: becomes author insert, magically fixes everything, patronizes locals
- Subversion: the newcomer is wrong about what this place needs; they must learn, not teach

**The Elder/Keeper of Memory**
- Remembers when, knows why
- Living archive of place-history
- What they offer: depth, continuity, the long view
- Danger: becomes dispensable font of wisdom, dies for emotional impact
- Subversion: their memory is selective, their stories are incomplete; they're protecting something

**The Quiet One**
- Says little, sees everything
- Noticed only by those paying attention
- What they offer: observer perspective, unspoken depth, reward for attentive readers
- Danger: becomes mystery-bait without interiority
- Subversion: their quietness isn't depth—it's avoidance, shyness, or loss they can't language yet

### Temporal Tropes

**The Seasonal Celebration**
- Festival, holiday, annual tradition
- Community gathered, past and present touching
- What it offers: structural anchor, heightened stakes, memory trigger
- Danger: becomes Hallmark set-dressing
- Subversion: the celebration that almost didn't happen, the one nobody wanted to organize this year, the first one without someone

**The Daily Ritual**
- Morning coffee, evening walk, weekly call
- Small acts that structure existence
- What they offer: characterization through action, grounding specificity
- Danger: becomes repetitive scenery
- Subversion: the ritual disrupted—what happens when the cafe closes early? When the walking path is blocked? The self who emerges when the script fails

**The Generational Handoff**
- Recipe passed down, business inherited, tradition transmitted
- What remains, what changes in the translation
- What it offers: continuity, stakes of legacy, bittersweet transition
- Danger: becomes sentimental obligation
- Subversion: the inheritance refused, the tradition deliberately broken, the younger generation's right to choose differently

### Relationship Tropes

**Found Family**
- Chosen bonds stronger than blood
- The people who became home
- What it offers: earned intimacy, loyalty without obligation, readers' wish fulfillment
- Danger: becomes uncomplicated; found families have conflict too
- Subversion: the found family is tested—someone leaves, betrays, needs more than the group can give

**Small Kindnesses**
- The anonymous gift, the held door, the remembered preference
- Love expressed in action, not declaration
- What they offer: characterization, community texture, emotional warmth without sentiment
- Danger: becomes twee
- Subversion: the kindness misread, the help not wanted, the care that smothers

**The Neighborhood**
- Proximity as relationship foundation
- What you owe those who live beside you
- What it offers: organic community, low-stakes drama, diverse connections
- Danger: becomes sitcom setup
- Subversion: the neighbor conflict that doesn't resolve, the fence that means more than property

## Style and Voice

### Prose Texture

Slice of life prose tends toward:
- Present-tense awareness (what's happening now, exactly now)
- Sensory specificity over abstraction (the smell of this bread, not bread in general)
- Longer sentences that breathe, shorter ones for moments that matter
- Accumulation of detail that builds mood rather than speeds plot
- Quiet observation, gentle noticing
- Space on the page—room for reader reflection

**Cultivate:**
- Precision about the physical world
- Seasonal markers embedded naturally
- The weight of routine, the surprise of disruption
- Interior moments that don't announce themselves as profound

**Avoid:**
- Manufactured drama where stillness would serve better
- Telling readers what to feel about mundane moments
- Generic sensory language (the smell of coffee, the warmth of sun—which coffee? which sun?)
- Forced significance, underlining what should accumulate

### Dialogue Considerations

**Rooted locals might:**
- Speak in shorthand, finish each other's sentences
- Reference shared history without explanation
- Communicate through silence and glance as much as words
- Have catchphrases, verbal tics that mark them

**Newcomers might:**
- Ask the wrong questions, miss the implications
- Over-explain, under-assume
- Use vocabulary that marks them as outsider
- Slowly adopt local rhythms without noticing

**Elders might:**
- Tell stories that aren't quite relevant
- Speak in proverbs they don't know are proverbs
- Remember aloud, narrate the past into present
- Leave space for what they're not saying

**Children/Young people might:**
- Ask obvious questions that reveal hidden tensions
- Miss context, catch subtext
- Speak the unspeakable because they don't know not to

### Sensory Palette

Slice of life engages:
- **Touch:** the worn smooth of a wooden counter, temperature of the first cold day, flour on hands
- **Scent:** seasonal markers (cut grass, wood smoke, rain on pavement), food as emotional shorthand
- **Sound:** the bell on the door, ambient layers of a place, what silence sounds like here
- **Taste:** the meal that means home, the failed recipe, the first bite of the seasonal thing
- **Proprioception:** the body's knowledge of a space, how you move through places you know by heart

## Pacing Expectations

### Anti-Climax as Feature

Slice of life inverts traditional pacing:
- Tension builds not toward explosion but toward... a conversation. A realization. A choice made quietly.
- The "climax" might be: finally asking for help. Admitting something aloud. Deciding to stay or go.
- Catharsis comes from accumulation, not incident—the 20th cup of coffee in the same chair is the story.

### Episodic Structure

- Self-contained scenes/chapters that could stand alone
- Each episode illuminates the whole without requiring it
- Callbacks to earlier moments create depth, not plot
- "What happens next" matters less than "what does this mean"

### Seasonal/Cyclical Movement

Common structural shapes:
1. **A Year In...** — Following a place/community through four seasons
2. **Before They Leave** — The summer before college, the weeks before the move
3. **The Return** — Coming back to a place changed by leaving and by time
4. **The Newcomer's First Year** — Learning a place from outside-in
5. **The Keeper's Final Year** — The last year of the shop, the last harvest, the final season

### Rhythm and Breath

- Scenes breathe—they're allowed to take time
- The pace of real life: waiting, anticipating, remembering
- Transitions matter—the walk between places, the moment before entering
- Not every scene advances something; some scenes are presence

## Common Pitfalls

**Nothing Happens (Actually)**
- Problem: mistaking lack of plot for slice of life
- Fix: things do happen—they're small, specific, and meaningful to these characters. If readers don't care whether the bakery gets the wedding order right, you haven't made the bakery real enough.

**Saccharine Without Substance**
- Problem: all warmth, no texture; cozy without complexity
- Fix: contentment exists alongside grief, frustration, loneliness. The gathering place has a broken chair nobody fixes. The cheerful baker can't sleep.

**Nostalgia Trap**
- Problem: romanticized past, simplified present, no real engagement with difficulty
- Fix: slice of life can be set now, can acknowledge that community is also surveillance, that belonging is also constraint, that tradition is also stagnation.

**The Interchangeable Cozy**
- Problem: generic coffee shop, bookstore, small town that could be any of a thousand
- Fix: specificity. This place, this community, these people. What's true here that's true nowhere else?

**Conflict Avoidance**
- Problem: everyone's nice, tensions resolve too easily, no friction
- Fix: slice of life isn't conflict-free—it's low-stakes conflict. The fight over how to decorate for the festival matters. Let characters disagree, disappoint each other, make mistakes.

**The Tourist Gaze**
- Problem: treating community/place as aesthetic consumption, not lived experience
- Fix: include friction, labor, maintenance. Someone has to clean the charming cafe at closing time.

**Mistaking Slow for Deep**
- Problem: pace is languid but nothing accumulates; scenes exist but don't resonate
- Fix: every scene should deposit something that pays off later. The passing detail becomes the crucial one.

## Cozy Design

What creates warmth, comfort, belonging—the architecture of coziness:

### Environmental Elements

- **Threshold rituals:** the bell on the door, removing shoes, the greeting that's always the same
- **Temperature contrast:** cold outside, warm inside; the relief of arrival
- **Ambient sound layers:** background music, kitchen noise, rain on windows, quiet conversation
- **Light quality:** lamp light over fluorescent, natural light that changes through the day
- **Worn surfaces:** the smooth of handling, the patina of use, things made better by time
- **Scent anchors:** baking, coffee, wood, old books—smells that signal safety

### Social Elements

- **The Usual:** being known enough that you don't have to order
- **Saved spots:** the chair that's yours, the corner people know is taken
- **Remembered preferences:** the barista who starts making your drink when you walk in
- **Rituals of care:** the extra cookie, the refilled water, the "how's your mother?"
- **Comfortable silence:** people who can be together without speaking
- **Reliable presence:** knowing who'll be there Thursday afternoon

### Narrative Elements

- **Repeated spaces:** returning to the same location, noticing small changes
- **Seasonal markers:** the decorations change, the menu shifts, the light different
- **Callbacks:** the detail from chapter 2 that returns in chapter 15
- **Accumulated meaning:** the first visit vs. the twentieth, same place, different weight
- **The long exhale:** scenes that end in settling, in comfort, in finally-home

When extracting character voice (Phase 6b), add these dimensions:

```yaml
voice:
  # Standard fields plus:

  rootedness_markers:
    local_vocabulary: [words/phrases specific to this place]
    shorthand_references: [shared history invoked without explanation]
    geographic_knowledge: [the back way, the shortcut, where to go when]

  rhythms:
    daily_patterns: "how their day structures their speech (morning gruffness, afternoon ease)"
    seasonal_shifts: "how their voice changes with the time of year"
    routine_phrases: [things they always say, verbal habits that mark them]

  silence_patterns:
    comfortable_with: [topics they don't need to speak about]
    avoiding: [topics their silence signals]
    expressive_silences: "what their pauses mean"
```

## Integration Notes

**Combine with other modules:**
- **+ Mystery (Cozy Mystery):** A crime in the community—the amateur detective knows everyone, low violence, the puzzle matters more than danger
- **+ Fantasy (Cozy Fantasy):** Low-stakes fantasy in a cottage, shop, or village; magic is domestic, threat minimal, found family core
- **+ Romance:** Slow-burn in a small community; everyone has opinions; the first date at the only restaurant in town
- **+ Horror (Subtle):** Something wrong in the comfortable place; the coziness is mask or menaced
- **+ Coming of Age:** The last summer before everything changes; the town you outgrow

**Integration considerations:**
- When adding conflict genres, preserve the texture of daily life—the mystery is solved between lunch rushes
- Cozy fantasy/mystery keep the emotional register of slice of life even with fantastical/suspenseful elements
- The gathering place often remains central even when plot pulls elsewhere

- `entities.yaml` → Add `rootedness_markers`, `rhythms`, `silence_patterns` to voice
