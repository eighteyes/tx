## Genre Identity

These are conventions, not commandments. Know the expectations so you can meet or subvert them intentionally.

Romance typically treats relationship as architecture, not subplot. The central question is often "will these two people find their way to each other?" rather than "will the world be saved?"—though everything else—war, mystery, career, family—can serve to pressure-test the relationship.

**Core tensions:**
- Vulnerability vs. self-protection
- Desire vs. fear of rejection
- Independence vs. partnership
- Past wounds vs. present possibility
- What I want vs. what I deserve

**Emotional register:**
- Yearning as sustenance
- Joy alongside ache
- Hope that terrifies
- Anger that reveals care
- Tenderness as revolution

**The Contract:**
Many romance readers expect emotional payoff. HEA (Happily Ever After) or HFN (Happy For Now) represent one path—but tragic romance exists and subversion is valid. When you choose to upend the happy ending, let the journey earn that choice too.

## Extraction Questions

Weave these into the core phases as appropriate.

### Relationship Foundation

**The Meet:**
- "How do these characters first encounter each other? What's the emotional charge?"
- "What's each character's first impression? What do they get wrong?"
- "What detail does each notice that they shouldn't? That they can't stop thinking about?"
- "Is there immediate attraction, immediate antagonism, or both?"

**The Stakes:**
- "What does each character risk by falling in love?"
- "What are they protecting by staying closed off?"
- "What would they lose if this works? What would they lose if it doesn't?"
- "What do they want vs. what do they need?"

**The Compatibility:**
- "Where do they fit together perfectly?"
- "Where do they grind against each other?"
- "What can they give each other that no one else can?"
- "What do they see in the other that the other doesn't see in themselves?"

### Internal Obstacles

**Wounds and Walls:**
- "What past relationship—romantic, familial, friendship—shaped their fear?"
- "What lie do they believe about love? About themselves?"
- "What's the thing they'll never do again? (Until they do.)"
- "How do they sabotage connection before it can hurt them?"

**Growth Arc:**
- "What must each character learn to deserve this love?"
- "What behavior must they unlearn?"
- "What do they need to forgive—in themselves, in others?"
- "When do they choose the relationship over their defense mechanisms?"

### External Obstacles

**Circumstantial Pressure:**
- "What forces keep them apart? Distance? Timing? Obligation?"
- "Who disapproves? Why does that disapproval matter?"
- "What external deadline or threat accelerates the relationship?"
- "What would make walking away the 'sensible' choice?"

**The Rival (if present):**
- "Is there a third party? Are they a genuine threat or a clarifying contrast?"
- "What does the rival offer that the love interest seemingly can't?"
- "How does the rival reveal what the protagonist truly values?"

### Chemistry Markers

**Physical Awareness:**
- "What does each character notice about the other's body? Voice? Movement?"
- "What accidental touches carry voltage?"
- "What's the first physical contact that means something?"

**Emotional Intimacy:**
- "When do they first laugh together genuinely?"
- "What secret does one trust the other with first?"
- "When do they see each other at their worst—and stay?"

## Trope Catalog

Tropes are contracts with readers. Know which you're signing and how you're honoring—or subverting—the terms.

### Relationship Origin Tropes

**Enemies to Lovers**
- Conflict creates heat; opposition forces engagement
- Requires genuine antagonism, not misunderstanding
- Danger: hate-to-love without showing the transformation
- Danger: "enemies" who are just mean for no reason
- Subversion: they were never enemies—they were both fighting the same thing from different angles

**Friends to Lovers**
- Foundation of trust, risk of ruining what exists
- The ache of wanting more while having something precious
- Danger: no romantic tension, just "now we kiss"
- Danger: the friendship feels thin—they're already acting like lovers
- Subversion: one always knew; the realization isn't mutual—it's overdue

**Strangers to Lovers**
- Pure discovery, no baggage, no context
- Requires building trust from zero
- Danger: falls flat without establishing why THESE strangers
- Subversion: they're not strangers—one remembers, one doesn't

**Second Chance Romance**
- History as weight and shortcut; they know each other's worst
- Requires addressing why it failed before
- Danger: repeating the original conflict without growth
- Danger: the original breakup was too unforgivable
- Subversion: they're different people now—meeting again, not reconciling

### Circumstance Tropes

**Forced Proximity**
- Can't escape each other—stuck together by situation
- Physical closeness accelerates emotional reckoning
- Danger: contrivance shows; "why don't they just leave?"
- Subversion: the proximity isn't the force—it's the excuse they both needed

**Fake Dating / Marriage of Convenience**
- Pretending becomes real; performance reveals truth
- The audience (in-world) believes it before they do
- Danger: the fake arrangement has no stakes
- Subversion: one was never pretending

**Only One Bed**
- Intimacy imposed by circumstance
- Physical proximity with negotiated boundaries
- Danger: used as cheap excuse for contact
- Subversion: they've shared beds before—this time it's different

**Forbidden Love**
- External forces declare the relationship impossible
- Choosing love means choosing consequences
- Danger: the "forbidden" element is arbitrary or easily solved
- Subversion: the forbidden-ness is internalized—the real barrier is belief they don't deserve it

**Love Triangle**
- Not about choosing between people—about choosing between selves
- Each option represents a different future, different identity
- Danger: one option is clearly wrong; no real tension
- Danger: protagonist seems selfish or indecisive
- Subversion: the triangle resolves not by choosing, but by one party choosing themselves

### Dynamic Tropes

**Slow Burn**
- Tension stretched to breaking point
- Every small touch carries accumulated weight
- Requires: enough content to justify the burn; payoff proportional to wait
- Danger: burns so slow it goes cold
- Calibration: know your story length—shorts can't slow-burn; serials thrive on it

**Insta-Love (Insta-Attraction)**
- Immediate recognition, cosmic pull
- Works when: acknowledged as alarming, explored rather than assumed
- Danger: feels unearned, skips the work readers crave
- Subversion: the instant love is one-sided; the other must catch up

**Grumpy/Sunshine**
- Opposites in temperament; the grump softens, the sunshine finds depth
- Light reveals shadow; shadow gives light somewhere to land
- Danger: one-dimensional contrast; grump is just mean, sunshine is just naive
- Subversion: the sunshine is performing; the grump is protecting

**Hurt/Comfort**
- Vulnerability creates intimacy; care creates bond
- One wounded, one tending
- Danger: pain exists only for comfort payoff; feels manipulative
- Subversion: the comfort-giver needs comfort; the wounded one provides it

**Mutual Pining**
- Both want; neither acts; tension is exquisite torture
- Requires: reader knowledge that both are pining
- Danger: frustration becomes annoyance; "just TALK to each other"
- Subversion: they're not pining—they're waiting for the other to be ready

## Style and Voice

### Prose Texture

Romance prose operates on:
- Interiority—we live in character heads, feeling what they feel
- Body awareness—heart rate, breath, skin temperature, space between bodies
- Metaphor for emotion—weather, sensation, memory
- Sensory specificity—the particular, not the generic

**Dialogue as Foreplay:**
- Subtext carries weight; what's NOT said vibrates
- Banter as intimacy; verbal sparring as compatibility test
- The shift from banter to sincerity—that catches the breath
- Questions that aren't questions; answers that reveal

**Internal Monologue:**
- The argument with oneself about what that look meant
- Noticing, then scolding oneself for noticing
- The rationalization that fails
- Hope and dread intertwined

**Body Language as Language:**
- Micro-expressions that contradict words
- Involuntary responses—pupils, flush, stillness
- The body that betrays the guarded heart
- Space closed, space maintained, space charged

### Sensory Palette

Romance engages:
- **Touch:** accidental contact, deliberate restraint, the almost-touch
- **Sound:** tone of voice, the way they say your name, breath catching
- **Sight:** watching when they don't know you're watching; being watched
- **Scent:** cologne, skin, familiar and foreign
- **Taste:** shared food, first kiss, tears

### Heat Level Considerations

**Closed door:** Emotion carries the intimacy; fade to black after threshold
**Warm:** Sensuality present; suggestion without explicit description
**Steamy:** Physical intimacy detailed; emotional connection maintained
**Explicit:** Full detail; intimacy serves character and relationship, not just sensation

Regardless of heat level: physical intimacy should reveal character. It's not intermission from the story—it IS story.

## Pacing Expectations

### The Romance Arc

Standard romance structure:
1. **Inciting Connection** — they meet; something shifts
2. **Resistance** — one or both fight the pull; denial stage
3. **Building** — forced interaction; walls develop cracks
4. **Turning Point** — first vulnerability; the relationship can't go backward
5. **Deepening** — intimacy (emotional and/or physical) escalates
6. **The 80% Crisis** — all seems lost; the relationship is threatened
7. **Grand Gesture / Choice** — active decision to fight for the relationship
8. **Resolution** — HEA or HFN; new equilibrium

### Beat Distribution

**First 25%:** Setup, meet-cute or meet-clash, establish stakes and obstacles
**25-50%:** Building tension, forced proximity, walls cracking, first kiss territory
**50-75%:** Deepening, vulnerability, intimacy escalation, relationship feels possible
**75-90%:** Crisis—external or internal threat, the Black Moment, all seems lost
**90-100%:** Resolution, grand gesture, commitment, emotional payoff

### The Almost-Kiss

Romance's signature beat. Use it wisely:
- Interruption creates ache
- Near-miss builds pressure
- The longer the delay, the bigger the payoff required
- Eventually, you must deliver—or readers revolt

### The Black Moment (80% Crisis)

The relationship's lowest point. Types:
- **External:** circumstance tears them apart (departure, revelation, threat)
- **Internal:** wound flares, old patterns resurface, fear wins temporarily
- **Betrayal:** real or perceived; trust shattered
- **Sacrifice:** one leaves "for the other's good"

Resolution must address ROOT CAUSE, not just reunite bodies.

## Common Pitfalls

**Miscommunication as Only Obstacle**
- Problem: everything would be solved if they just talked
- Fix: make the obstacle REAL—talking wouldn't actually solve it
- Or: make the inability to communicate CHARACTER-based and address THAT

**Told, Not Shown Chemistry**
- Problem: narrator says they have chemistry; reader doesn't feel it
- Fix: show them sparking; banter, glances, awareness, compatibility
- Test: cover declarations—does the relationship exist in the interactions?

**Conflict Without Character**
- Problem: obstacles are external and arbitrary; characters are perfect
- Fix: the obstacle should emerge from who they ARE, not just what happens to them
- The flaw that keeps them apart is the flaw they must grow past

**The Too-Perfect Love Interest**
- Problem: love interest exists only to love the protagonist; no interiority
- Fix: give them wants, fears, flaws, a life beyond this relationship
- They should be someone a reader could imagine without the protagonist

**Trauma as Decoration**
- Problem: past pain mentioned but never integrated; used for sympathy points
- Fix: the wound must SHAPE present behavior; healing must be WORK
- The breakthrough must cost something and change something

**No Reason for THESE Two**
- Problem: any two attractive people could be in this story
- Fix: make their connection specific; what can only THEY give each other?
- Why is this person the key to the other's lock?

**Rushed Resolution**
- Problem: 200 pages of conflict, 5 pages of resolution
- Fix: the getting-together is a destination worth landing in
- Show the new normal; let readers FEEL the payoff

**Grand Gesture Without Growth**
- Problem: big romantic moment, but character hasn't actually changed
- Fix: the gesture must demonstrate the internal shift, not replace it
- What they DO should only be possible because of who they've BECOME

## Character Depth Extensions

Genre-specific entity fields for character files. The calibrator adds these sections based on active genre modules.

### Extraction Questions

**Sexuality and Romantic Identity:**
- "What's their orientation? Their history with romance—patterns they've fallen into, lessons they've learned or ignored?"
- "What's the pattern in who they choose? The type they're drawn to vs. the type that's good for them?"
- "What do they actually like in intimacy—emotional, physical, both? What matters most to them?"
- "What's the gap between how they talk about sex/romance and how they actually feel about it?"

**New Relationship Energy (NRE):**
- "How does falling feel for them? Giddy? Terrifying? A mix? What does their body do when they're around the person?"
- "What reckless thing might they do when swept up? What boundaries blur? What clarity do they lose?"
- "How does NRE change their mental landscape? Do they obsess? Catastrophize? Float?"

**Romantic Desires:**
- "What do they want from a relationship that they've never admitted out loud?"
- "What's the fantasy they'd be embarrassed to name? The emotional need underneath the surface wants?"
- "What would make them feel truly seen, truly safe, truly chosen?"

**Intimacy Architecture:**
- "How do they build toward physical closeness—fast, slow, chaotic, calculated?"
- "What's their comfort level with vulnerability? Do they lead with body or heart? Which scares them more?"
- "What makes them pull back? What makes them lean in despite fear?"

**Attraction Markers (from original voice section):**
- "What do they notice about the love interest first? What detail keeps returning to their thoughts?"
- "How do they try NOT to notice? What do they scold themselves for paying attention to?"
- "When does attraction show despite their control? Physical tells, verbal slips, the mask that cracks?"

**Vulnerability Patterns:**
- "What makes their walls drop? What kind of moment or statement undoes their defenses?"
- "How do they rebuild walls when scared? Humor? Distance? Anger? Going cold?"
- "What are their tells for genuine emotion? What can't they hide no matter how hard they try?"

**Intimacy Voice Shift:**
- "How does their voice change in intimate moments vs. public ones? What formality drops? What emerges?"
- "What do they call the love interest when alone vs. with others? What words are private?"
- "How do they look at the person when they think no one's watching? What shifts when they're observed?"

**Banter Patterns:**
- "What's their style of verbal play with the love interest? Teasing? Wit? Absurdity? Provocation?"
- "What topics are safe for sparring? What subjects carry too much charge to joke about?"
- "Where's the line where banter becomes too real? What truth hiding in jest would shatter the game?"

## Example Seeds

Seeds that work well in romance:

**Meet-Cute / Meet-Clash:**
- "The drink they spilled on you—and what you said"
- "The argument that started in public and didn't end"
- "The favor you didn't want to ask; the yes that surprised you"
- "Mistaken for someone else—and the mistake that followed"

**Tension and Awareness:**
- "The song that was playing when you first touched"
- "What you noticed about their hands before you noticed anything else"
- "The joke only you two understand"
- "The question you've never answered honestly—until them"
- "A voicemail never deleted"

**Vulnerability and Intimacy:**
- "What you told them at 3am that you've told no one else"
- "The first time you saw them cry"
- "The scar and the story they finally told you"
- "The fear you admitted; the way they didn't fix it"
- "A promise made in the dark"

**Conflict and Yearning:**
- "The fight that revealed how much you cared"
- "The goodbye you didn't mean"
- "What you almost said in the doorway"
- "The letter you wrote and never sent—until now"
- "The last time you saw each other—before this"

**Mundane Intimacy:**
- "Their coffee order memorized before you meant to"
- "The hoodie you never returned"
- "Falling asleep in the same room—nothing else, just that"
- "A grocery store, a cart, the beginning of 'we'"
- "The first time you used 'we' and meant it"

## Integration Notes

**Combine with other modules:**
- **+ Dark Fantasy:** monstrous love interests, immortal yearning, forbidden bonds
- **+ Mystery/Thriller:** partnership under pressure, trust earned through danger
- **+ Comedy:** romcom pacing, humorous obstacles, lighter touch
- **+ Historical:** period-appropriate constraints, societal obstacles, epistolary elements
- **+ Sci-Fi:** long-distance across space, AI/human love, time-crossed lovers

**Extract to:**
- `setting.yaml` → Add social constraints on relationships, matchmaking customs, marriage politics if relevant
- `entities.yaml` → Add `attraction_markers`, `vulnerability_patterns`, `intimacy_voice_shift`, `banter_patterns` to voice
- `arc.yaml` → Include relationship beats: meet, turning points, crisis, resolution in seeds
- `pacing.yaml` → Mark romance-specific beats: almost-kiss moments, vulnerability reveals, black moment, HEA/HFN

**Subgenre Calibrations:**
- **Contemporary:** real-world stakes, relatable obstacles, grounded voice
- **Historical:** period accuracy, restricted contact, letters and longing
- **Paranormal:** add dark-fantasy module, adjust for immortal stakes
- **Romantic Suspense:** danger accelerates intimacy, trust under fire
- **Romantic Comedy:** lighter voice, humor-driven obstacles, faster pacing to first kiss
