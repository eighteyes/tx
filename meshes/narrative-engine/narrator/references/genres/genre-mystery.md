## Genre Identity

**These are conventions, not commandments.** Know the expectations so you can meet or subvert them intentionally.

Mystery traditionally centers on controlled revelation. Every scene is a transaction: what does the reader learn, what do they think they learned, and what remains hidden? The detective (or reader) assembles fragments into truth. Many readers expect this covenant: the solution was always available to the attentive.

**Core tensions (often present):**
- Truth vs. deception (and which characters embody each?)
- Order vs. chaos (the crime disrupts; the solution restores)
- Knowledge vs. ignorance (power through information asymmetry)
- Justice vs. law (are they the same thing?)
- Observation vs. assumption (the reader's journey)

**Typical emotional registers:**
- Curiosity more than fear
- Satisfaction more than catharsis
- Unease that resolves into clarity
- The pleasure of pattern recognition

## Extraction Questions

Weave these into the core phases as appropriate.

### World-Bones Additions

**The Information Landscape:**
- "What systems exist for uncovering truth? (Law enforcement, private inquiry, community networks)"
- "Who has access to information others don't? What gives them that access?"
- "What's the relationship between official investigation and unofficial?"
- "What information is forbidden, classified, or taboo to seek?"

**The Nature of Crime:**
- "What constitutes transgression in this world? Legal vs. moral crimes?"
- "What motivates crime here? Passion, profit, ideology, necessity?"
- "What are the consequences of crime—official and social?"
- "Who falls outside the protection of law? Who's above it?"

**Truth Mechanics:**
- "How reliable is evidence in this world? (Forensics, testimony, documentation)"
- "What methods exist for deception? What makes them succeed or fail?"
- "Are there supernatural/technological means of truth-detection? Limits?"
- "What's the relationship between truth and proof?"

### Character Additions

**For Detective Characters:**
- "What gives them the right to investigate? Official authority, personal stake, skill?"
- "What do they notice that others miss? What's their cognitive signature?"
- "What's their relationship with rules? Do they bend them? Break them?"
- "What personal blind spots compromise their observation?"
- "What motivates them beyond the case? Justice? Curiosity? Ego? Penance?"
- "What would make them walk away from a case? What wouldn't?"

**For Suspects/Persons of Interest:**
- "What are they hiding—related to the crime or not?"
- "What do they fear the investigation will expose?"
- "What's their relationship to the victim?"
- "What's their relationship to truth-telling? Habitual liars? Compulsively honest?"

**For Victims:**
- "What made them a target? Specific threat or wrong place/time?"
- "What secrets did they carry? Which are relevant?"
- "Who benefits from their silence? Their death? Their absence?"
- "What did they know that they shouldn't have?"

### The Hidden Architecture

- "What is actually hidden? (The 'what' of the mystery)"
- "Who knows the truth? Partial truth? Lies they believe are true?"
- "Where are the clues distributed? Physical, testimonial, documented?"
- "What is the reader meant to suspect incorrectly?"
- "When should the reader be able to solve it? (Before reveal, at reveal, never quite)"

## Trope Catalog

Tropes are tools, not sins. Know which you're invoking and which you're subverting.

### Detective Archetypes

**The Amateur Sleuth**
- Civilian with curiosity and access (writer, antique dealer, caterer)
- Stumbles into crime through circumstance or community
- Advantage: goes where police can't, sees what officials miss
- Danger: becomes implausibly competent, always present at murders
- Subversion: their amateur status is the point—fresh eyes catch what routine misses

**The Hardboiled Private Eye**
- Mean streets, moral complexity, personal code
- Operates in the grey between law and lawless
- Advantage: moves between worlds, beholden to client not state
- Danger: nihilistic posturing, toxic masculinity as aesthetic
- Subversion: the cynical exterior cracks to reveal genuine moral conviction

**The Cozy Detective**
- Small community, contained stakes, violence at arm's length
- Restoration of social order as primary goal
- Advantage: deep community knowledge, relationship-based detection
- Danger: death becomes too comfortable, sanitized violence
- Subversion: the cozy setting conceals genuine darkness; small towns have deep secrets

**The Police Procedural Investigator**
- Institutional resources and constraints
- Detection as labor, teamwork, bureaucracy
- Advantage: realistic process, multiple perspectives
- Danger: procedure overwhelms character, becomes formula
- Subversion: the institution is part of the problem; solving requires breaking from protocol

**The Armchair Detective**
- Solves through pure reasoning, minimal footwork
- Information comes to them; they synthesize
- Advantage: showcases deduction, reader can follow the logic
- Danger: becomes passive, other characters reduced to information delivery
- Subversion: their removal from the world blinds them to human elements

**The Reluctant Detective**
- Doesn't want to investigate; circumstances demand it
- Personal stakes force involvement
- Advantage: emotional investment, reader sympathy
- Danger: contrivance required to keep them involved
- Subversion: the reluctance reveals something they're afraid to discover about themselves

### Mystery Structure Types

**Whodunit**
- Identity of culprit is the core puzzle
- Multiple viable suspects, one solution
- Advantage: classic structure, clear puzzle
- Danger: character reduced to suspicion quotient
- Subversion: the "who" matters less than the "why" once revealed

**Howdunit**
- Impossible crime, mechanical puzzle
- Locked room, perfect alibi, vanishing weapon
- Advantage: intellectual satisfaction, ingenuity on display
- Danger: becomes magic trick without emotional stakes
- Subversion: the "how" reveals character—the method exposes the mind

**Whydunit**
- Motive is the mystery; identity may be known
- Psychological depth, character study
- Advantage: emotional resonance, complex characters
- Danger: becomes literary fiction wearing mystery costume
- Subversion: the motive inverts expectations—victim and culprit roles blur

**Inverted Mystery (Columbo Structure)**
- Reader knows culprit from start; detection is the show
- How will they be caught? What mistake will undo them?
- Advantage: dramatic irony, character focus on both sides
- Danger: foregone conclusion drains tension
- Subversion: the detective's methods are morally questionable—who's really the antagonist?

**Open Mystery**
- Multiple possible solutions, interpretation invited
- Ambiguity as feature, not bug
- Advantage: literary depth, reader engagement
- Danger: frustrates readers expecting closure
- Subversion: the ambiguity IS the point—some truths can't be proven

### Misdirection Tropes

**Red Herring**
- False clue that leads investigation astray
- Must be plausible but ultimately irrelevant
- Danger: feels like cheating if not rooted in character/plot truth
- Proper use: the herring exists for a reason beyond misdirection

**The McGuffin**
- Object everyone pursues; specifics don't matter
- Motivates action, not resolution
- Danger: if mystery hinges on it, resolution feels empty
- Proper use: what the McGuffin reveals about pursuers is the real mystery

**The Locked Room**
- Impossible crime: no entry, no exit, yet it happened
- Mechanical puzzle with logical solution
- Danger: solution more interesting than human stakes
- Proper use: the impossibility reflects psychological truth

**The Unreliable Witness**
- Testimony that misleads—deliberately or through limitation
- Perception, memory, motivation all compromise truth
- Danger: too many unreliable sources frustrates more than intrigues
- Proper use: their unreliability reveals character, not just plot

**The Hidden Connection**
- Seemingly unrelated elements link crucially
- Requires careful foreshadowing
- Danger: connection feels arbitrary if not properly seeded
- Proper use: the connection, once seen, recontextualizes everything

**The Double Bluff**
- Obvious suspect is too obvious... or is that what we're meant to think?
- Layers of suspicion and dismissal
- Danger: becomes exhausting, reader stops trusting anything
- Proper use: sparingly, and the resolution honors the complexity

## Style and Voice

### Prose Texture

Mystery prose tends toward:
- Precision in physical description (details matter; train the reader's eye)
- Observation as characterization (what the detective notices reveals their mind)
- Strategic ambiguity (syntax that means two things)
- Controlled information release (every sentence a choice about revelation)
- Atmosphere through specific detail, not adjective accumulation

**Avoid:**
- Withholding information the POV character possesses
- Describing clues the detective "notices" without showing them
- Telegraphing suspicion through obvious narrative emphasis
- Purple prose that obscures rather than reveals

### The Information Drip

**Feeding the Reader:**
- Clues embedded in scene, not announced
- Important information surrounded by plausible irrelevance
- Repetition signals significance (if handled subtly)
- The reader should be able to notice what the detective notices—but not necessarily interpret correctly

**The Fair Play Covenant:**
- The solution must be achievable from provided information
- Critical clues must appear before revelation
- The detective can't know things the reader doesn't
- Coincidence can initiate but not resolve

### Detective Voice

**First-Person Detective:**
- Reader's access limited to detective's observations
- Internal monologue reveals reasoning process
- Danger: can't show scenes detective doesn't witness
- Advantage: deep identification, shared discovery

**Third-Person Limited:**
- Camera over detective's shoulder
- Some interiority, more narrative flexibility
- Allows scenes detective learns of secondhand

**Third-Person Multiple:**
- Multiple perspectives, information asymmetry
- Reader may know more than any single character
- Danger: loses tight identification; can feel scattershot
- Advantage: dramatic irony, broader canvas

### Dialogue as Investigation

- Questions reveal as much as answers
- What isn't said; pauses and deflections
- Lies that contain truth; truth that misdirects
- Interview as character revelation (subject AND investigator)
- The detective's questioning style IS their characterization

## Pacing Expectations

### The Clue Rhythm

**Act One: The Corpse**
1. **Normalcy disrupted** — crime shatters status quo
2. **Investigation initiated** — detective engages
3. **Initial clues seeded** — reader receives first pieces
4. **Suspects introduced** — cast of possibilities assembled

**Act Two: The Chase**
5. **False trails pursued** — dead ends that teach
6. **Complications arise** — new crimes, new revelations
7. **Clues accumulate** — pattern begins emerging for attentive reader
8. **Mid-point revelation** — something changes the detective's understanding
9. **Stakes escalate** — danger, urgency, personal cost increase
10. **Dark night of investigation** — detective doubts, wrong path, failure

**Act Three: The Reveal**
11. **Final clue clicks** — the piece that completes the puzzle
12. **Confrontation** — detective faces truth
13. **Explanation** — solution presented (can be dramatic reveal or quiet realization)
14. **Resolution** — justice served, order restored (or pointedly not)

### False Solution Timing

- Plant potential solutions early; let reader commit
- The false solution should be satisfying enough to be believable
- Revelation of true solution should enhance, not invalidate, the journey
- The reader should think "I should have seen it" not "I couldn't have known"

### Revelation Mechanics

**The Drawing Room Reveal:**
- Classic gathering, dramatic accusation
- Works for: traditional, cozy, theatrical
- Danger: feels dated, requires contrivance to assemble

**The Quiet Realization:**
- Detective pieces it together; confronts individually
- Works for: hardboiled, psychological, literary
- Danger: can feel anticlimactic after complex build

**The Witnessed Confession:**
- Culprit reveals themselves through action or word
- Works for: thriller-mystery hybrids, procedural
- Danger: removes detective agency

**The Documentary Reveal:**
- Evidence speaks: letter, recording, document
- Works for: cold case, historical, epistolary
- Danger: passive, can feel deus ex machina

## Common Pitfalls

**The Cheat Reveal**
- Problem: solution depends on information withheld from reader
- Fix: every critical element must appear before the reveal, even if obscured

**The Too-Obvious Culprit**
- Problem: reader solves in chapter two; rest feels like waiting
- Fix: give the obvious suspect legitimate reasons to look guilty while guilty party hides in plain sight

**The Too-Obscure Culprit**
- Problem: culprit appears twice, has no development, pulled from nowhere
- Fix: guilty party needs presence, characterization, apparent motive for something

**The Clue Dump**
- Problem: all evidence in one scene; reader can't process or fails to engage
- Fix: distribute across scenes, embed in action and character, make discovery active

**The Idiot Detective**
- Problem: detective ignores obvious leads to extend plot
- Fix: genuine obstacles, misleading evidence, character-based blind spots

**The Omniscient Detective**
- Problem: detective knows things they shouldn't, solves without visible process
- Fix: show the work; confusion is permitted; wrong turns are human

**Motivation Deficit**
- Problem: culprit's motive doesn't justify the crime or the risk
- Fix: motive must be sufficient from culprit's perspective, even if irrational

**The Competence Vacuum**
- Problem: police/officials are stupid so amateur can shine
- Fix: give officials different priorities, jurisdictional limits, or information gaps—not incompetence

**Stakes Collapse**
- Problem: crime feels abstract; reader doesn't care about solution
- Fix: personal stakes for detective, genuine loss for survivors, threat of recurrence

## Fair Play Rules (Modernized)

Classical rules (Knox, Van Dine) updated for contemporary practice:

### The Covenant with the Reader

1. **All critical clues must be presented before the reveal.** The reader may miss them, but they must exist.

2. **The detective must not possess information the reader doesn't.** POV character's knowledge is reader's knowledge.

3. **The solution must be achievable through reason.** Intuition can guide; only logic can prove.

4. **The culprit must be a character with meaningful presence.** Introduced properly, developed sufficiently, present enough to be remembered.

5. **The method must be possible within the world's established rules.** No impossible poisons, unestablished technology, or supernatural intervention (unless the world permits it consistently).

6. **Coincidence may reveal crime but not solve it.** Lucky breaks open doors; they don't walk through them.

7. **The detective's reasoning must be shown, not just declared.** "I knew because I am brilliant" is not explanation.

8. **Romantic or professional subplots must not overshadow the puzzle.** They enrich; they don't replace.

9. **Narrative misdirection is permitted; authorial lying is not.** The prose may emphasize the wrong thing; it may not state falsehoods.

10. **The solution must be elegant relative to the complexity.** Simple crimes have simple solutions; byzantine plots may have elaborate answers—but the answer should feel proportionate.

### Fair Play in Practice

- Clues appear in scene, not exposition
- Detective's observations are specific enough for reader to follow
- Red herrings have internal logic (they mislead for character-based reasons)
- Timeline is clear enough to verify alibis
- Geography is consistent enough to track movement
- Character relationships are mapped sufficiently to understand motive

## Voice Modifiers for Mystery Characters

When extracting character voice (Phase 6b), add these dimensions:

```yaml
voice:
  # Standard fields plus:

  observation_style:
    notices_first: [what draws their attention initially]
    habitual_focus: [physical details, emotional tells, environmental factors]
    blind_spots: [what they consistently miss or dismiss]

  interview_approach:
    questioning_style: [direct, circuitous, sympathetic, confrontational]
    silence_use: [how they employ pauses, waiting, non-response]
    tell_reading: [what they watch for in others]

  information_handling:
    shares_freely: [true/false, with whom, what type]
    withholds: [what they keep, why]
    deception_comfort: [will they lie in service of truth?]

  reasoning_signature:
    deductive: [evidence to conclusion]
    inductive: [pattern to principle]
    abductive: [best explanation from incomplete data]
    intuitive: [leaps they make, how they justify]
```

## Example Seeds

Seeds that work well in mystery:

**Clue Seeds:**
- "The object that shouldn't be where it was found"
- "The alibi that's too perfect"
- "The witness who saw something they don't know they saw"
- "The routine that was broken on that day only"
- "The detail everyone mentions but no one examines"
- "The connection that only makes sense in retrospect"

**Character Seeds:**
- "The suspect who wants to be caught"
- "The liar who only tells the truth about one thing"
- "The person with no motive who confesses anyway"
- "The detective's blind spot made personal"
- "The victim no one mourns"
- "The innocent who looks guilty for unrelated reasons"

**Structural Seeds:**
- "Two crimes that seem connected but aren't"
- "The crime that reveals the older crime"
- "The solution everyone accepts that isn't true"
- "The investigation that makes things worse"
- "The truth that justice can't accommodate"
- "The case the detective wishes they hadn't solved"

**Atmosphere Seeds:**
- "The room where something happened that no one will discuss"
- "The anniversary that changes behavior"
- "The secret everyone knows but doesn't mention"
- "The new arrival who asks the wrong questions"
- "The record that was supposed to be destroyed"
- "The meeting that didn't happen but clearly did"

## Character Depth Extensions

Genre-specific entity fields for character files. The calibrator adds these sections based on active genre modules.

### Extraction Questions

**Observation Habits:**
- "What do they notice first when entering a room? People? Exits? Objects? Anomalies?"
- "How do they read people—micro-expressions, body language, what's not said?"
- "What details do they catalog automatically vs. what do they have to work to notice?"
- "What do they miss because of what they focus on? What blind spots does their method create?"

**Deduction Patterns:**
- "How do they solve problems—intuitive leaps, methodical analysis, or both in sequence?"
- "Do they think out loud or internally? Do they theorize early or wait for sufficient data?"
- "What's their eureka mechanics—how does the solution arrive? Flash of insight? Slow assembly?"
- "When they're wrong, what usually tripped them up? Assumption? Missing data? Emotional bias?"

**Trust Architecture:**
- "Who do they trust and why? What earns trust? What breaks it irrevocably?"
- "How paranoid are they? Do they suspect everyone or take people at face value?"
- "What's their default assumption about human nature—people are good, bad, self-interested, complex?"
- "Have they been betrayed? By whom? How did it change their trust patterns?"

**Information Hoarding:**
- "What do they keep to themselves and why? Strategic advantage? Protecting someone? Trust issues?"
- "When do they share information? What has to happen for them to reveal what they know?"
- "Who do they tell everything to, if anyone? Who gets the edited version?"
- "What piece of information are they sitting on right now that they shouldn't be?"

**Obsession Markers:**
- "What case defines them? The solved one? The unsolved one? The one that got personal?"
- "What can't they let go of? What thread do they keep pulling even when told to stop?"
- "What's the cost of their obsession? Relationships? Health? Boundaries? Sleep?"
- "When do they know to stop? Or do they never stop until the answer comes or breaks them?"

### Examples

**Observation Habits — Example:**
```yaml
observation_habits:
  first_notice: "Exits. Always. Where are the ways out, who's blocking them, what's the fastest route."
  reading_people: "Hands first. What people do with their hands tells more than faces. Watch for fidgeting, clenching, hiding."
  automatic_vs_effortful: "Automatically: body language, spatial relationships. Effortful: financial records, technical jargon, normal social cues."
  blind_spots: "Misses genuine emotion sometimes. So focused on deception that honesty looks suspicious."
```

**Deduction Patterns — Example:**
```yaml
deduction_patterns:
  problem_solving_style: "Methodical to a fault. Won't theorize until evidence supports it. Frustrated by partners who leap ahead."
  thinking_mode: "Internal. Needs silence to think. Talking disrupts the process. Will monologue solution once it's formed."
  eureka_mechanics: "Slow assembly. Like puzzle pieces clicking. The last piece snaps in and suddenly the whole picture's visible."
  usual_mistakes: "Overlooks the obvious because it's too simple. Looks for complexity and misses the straightforward answer."
```

**Trust Architecture — Example:**
```yaml
trust_architecture:
  who_they_trust: ["Dr. Rosen—twenty years of not lying", "No one else fully"]
  trust_earned_by: "Consistency over time. Doing the right thing when no one's watching. Admitting mistakes."
  trust_broken_by: "Lying to them directly. Betraying a confidence. Hurting someone they protected."
  paranoia_level: "Professionally paranoid. Everyone's a suspect until cleared. It's kept them alive."
  human_nature_default: "People are self-interested. They'll do good if it's convenient, bad if pressed. Predictable that way."
  betrayal_history: "Partner sold them out to close a case dirty. Took seven years to trust anyone again."
```

**Information Hoarding — Example:**
```yaml
information_hoarding:
  what_they_hide: "Hunches until proven. Connections to ongoing cases. Personal information about anyone."
  why_they_hide: "Strategic mostly. Information is currency. Share it too early and people adjust, hide, run."
  sharing_threshold: "When withholding would endanger someone. When they need another perspective. When directly asked by someone they trust."
  confidant: "Dr. Rosen gets everything. Everyone else gets what they need to know."
  current_secret: "Knows who the witness is protecting. Haven't told the captain because the captain would have to act on it."
```

**Obsession Markers — Example:**
```yaml
obsession_markers:
  defining_case: "The Bridgewater murders. Solved it. Killer's in prison. Still thinks they got the wrong person."
  cant_let_go: "The one inconsistency. The timeline doesn't work. Everyone says they're wrong. Can't stop checking."
  obsession_cost: "Marriage ended. Don't sleep well. Reputation as 'brilliant but difficult.' Worth it if they're right."
  stopping_point: "They don't stop. That's the problem. Follow threads until they break or the truth comes out."
```

## Integration Notes

**Combine with other modules:**
- **+ Thriller:** stakes escalate, detective becomes hunted, ticking clock
- **+ Horror:** the truth is monstrous; investigation uncovers existential threat
- **+ Romance:** relationship develops through investigation; trust as theme
- **+ Dark Fantasy:** the monster is the mystery; supernatural detection methods
- **+ Literary Fiction:** ambiguous resolution, character study, theme over puzzle

**Extract to:**
- `setting.yaml` → Add investigation infrastructure, information access rules, evidence handling
- `entities.yaml` → Add `observation_style`, `interview_approach`, `reasoning_signature` to detective voices
- `arc.yaml` → Include clue placement beats, revelation timing, false solution moments in seeds

**Subgenre Adjustments:**
- **Cozy:** soften violence, emphasize community, amateur detective, restorative justice
- **Noir:** moral ambiguity, institutional corruption, pyrrhic victories, hardboiled voice
- **Police Procedural:** institutional focus, team dynamics, realistic process
- **Locked Room:** mechanical puzzle emphasis, physical demonstration of solution
- **Psychological:** motive-focused, unreliable narration, internal investigation
- **Historical:** period-appropriate detection methods, different evidence standards
