# Rain, regret, and everybody's lying

## Genre Identity

These are conventions, not commandments. Know the expectations so you can meet or subvert them intentionally.

Noir typically isn't mystery wearing a trenchcoat. Mystery asks "whodunit." Noir often asks "does it even matter?" The crime is frequently solved; the soul typically remains unsaved.

Noir typically operates as tone, not plot type. It often carries the atmosphere that the fix is in, the game is rigged, and everyone—including the protagonist—is compromised. The detective often doesn't restore order; they survive its absence.

**What noir typically features:**
- A moral atmosphere, rather than a mystery framework
- Fatalism as a recurring philosophical undercurrent
- The city often functioning as organism (usually diseased)
- Voice that frequently becomes character itself
- Existential concerns dressed in noir aesthetics

**What noir often avoids:**
- Mystery with rain added as mere window dressing
- Cynicism as aesthetic without substance
- An excuse for nihilism without consequence
- Style that substitutes for story

**Core tensions noir typically explores:**
- Order vs. chaos (order's a lie, but chaos kills)
- Knowledge vs. innocence (knowing doesn't help)
- Justice vs. survival (pick one)
- Trust vs. self-preservation
- The personal code vs. a world without rules

**Emotional register noir typically emphasizes:**
- Exhaustion more than fear
- Bitterness more than anger
- Desire that knows better
- Humor as armor
- Occasional beauty in the wreckage

## Extraction Questions

Weave these into the core phases as appropriate.

### World-Bones Additions

**The Corruption Anatomy:**
- "What's rotting at the core of this city/world? How long has it been rotting?"
- "Who profits from the way things are? How visible is their hand?"
- "What still functions? What small decencies survive?"
- "How aware are ordinary people of the corruption? Do they accept it, ignore it, or fight it in small ways?"

**Power Architecture:**
- "Who really runs things? How many layers between them and the street?"
- "What's the relationship between official power and shadow power?"
- "Where does money flow? Where does it pool?"
- "What can't be bought? Does anything remain uncorrupted?"

**The Geography of Shadows:**
- "What does the city look like at 2 AM? Who's still awake?"
- "Where do secrets get exchanged? Where do bodies get found?"
- "What's the weather doing? (Rain isn't required, but atmosphere is)"
- "What's beautiful here despite everything? What's ugly despite pretensions?"

### Character Additions

**For the Protagonist:**
- "What broke them? Was it one moment or slow erosion?"
- "What's their personal code? What lines won't they cross?"
- "What vice keeps them company? What does it cost them?"
- "Why do they keep going? Spite? Stubbornness? One remaining thing worth protecting?"
- "What do they see that others miss? What do they refuse to see about themselves?"
- "Who did they used to be before they became this?"

**For the Femme Fatale / Homme Fatal:**
- "What's their actual goal beneath the surface play?"
- "Are they trapped or are they trapping? (Often both.)"
- "What would genuine feeling cost them in their current game?"
- "What made them this way? Is survival their only remaining ethic?"
- "Where's their moment of genuine vulnerability—if it exists?"

**For the Client / Mark / Patsy:**
- "What are they not telling? Why?"
- "Are they victim or architect of their situation?"
- "What would they sacrifice to get what they want?"
- "How much do they understand about the world they've stepped into?"

**For the Antagonist:**
- "How do they justify their corruption? Or do they even bother?"
- "What's the difference between them and the protagonist, really?"
- "What made them possible? What systems elevated them?"
- "Are they monstrous, banal, or something more unsettling: reasonable?"

### Relationship Dynamics

- "Who's using whom? Is it mutual?"
- "Where does attraction live alongside danger?"
- "What's the history between them? Noir loves history."
- "What can't be said aloud? What's communicated through silence, through action?"
- "If trust exists, what did it cost? What could shatter it?"

## Trope Catalog

Tropes are tools, not sins. Know which you're invoking and which you're subverting.

### Character Archetypes

**The Hardboiled Detective**
- Cynical but not empty; code-bound amid chaos
- Sees through everyone except perhaps themselves
- Danger: becomes parody, quip-machine with no interiority
- Subversion: the armor is thinner than they pretend; something still bruises them

**The Femme Fatale**
- Dangerous beauty with her own agenda
- Uses attraction as weapon, survival tool, or both
- Danger: becomes misogynistic caricature, punished for sexuality
- Subversion: she's the protagonist of her own noir; we just can't see it
- Subversion: genuine feeling isn't weakness; it's the one thing that might save her

**The Homme Fatal**
- The fatale's male counterpart (less common, equally potent)
- Charm that hides the knife
- Danger: becomes obvious villain
- Subversion: he believes his own lies

**The Corrupt Official**
- Police captain, politician, judge—the system's rot given a face
- Protection for the powerful, pressure on the vulnerable
- Danger: becomes cartoon villain
- Subversion: they're tired too; they remember when they had principles

**The Patsy / Fall Guy**
- Takes the blame, often dies for it
- Useful to everyone, protected by no one
- Danger: becomes mere plot device
- Subversion: the patsy sees everything; silence is their only power

**The Client Who Lies**
- The case that walks through the door is never the real case
- Their omissions shape everything
- Danger: twist becomes predictable
- Subversion: they're lying to protect someone who doesn't deserve it

### Situational Tropes

**The Double-Cross**
- Someone switches sides, often multiple times
- Loyalty was always conditional
- Danger: becomes exhausting shell game
- What it offers: tension, paranoia, the question of who can be trusted (often: no one)
- Subversion: the expected betrayal doesn't come; trust was earned after all

**The Setup / Frame**
- Protagonist is made to look guilty
- Must solve the case while hunted
- Danger: becomes contrived if evidence plants are too convenient
- What it offers: urgency, isolation, the system turned hostile
- Subversion: they were guilty of something—just not this

**The Past That Won't Stay Buried**
- Old case, old love, old sin returns
- What was done cannot be undone
- Danger: flashback-heavy structure loses momentum
- What it offers: depth, consequence, the weight of time
- Subversion: the past isn't what they remembered; memory lies too

**The Job That Goes Wrong**
- Simple task cascades into catastrophe
- One thread pulled, entire tapestry unravels
- Danger: complexity for its own sake
- What it offers: escalation, revelation, the sense that nothing is simple
- Subversion: it was supposed to go wrong; they were always the target

### Atmospheric Tropes

**Rain-Slicked Streets**
- Urban landscape as mood
- Reflections, obscured vision, the world washed but never clean
- Danger: becomes cliche if only decoration
- What it offers: atmosphere, metaphor for moral clarity dissolving
- Subversion: harsh sunlight noir—no shadows to hide in, nowhere to run

**Shadows and Smoke**
- Concealment, revelation, the space between knowing and seeing
- Cigarettes as props, punctuation, countdown to death
- Danger: aesthetic without substance
- What it offers: visual vocabulary, the sense that truth hides
- Subversion: the bright, clinical space where nothing hides but everything lies

**The Bottle**
- Whiskey, rye, whatever numbs
- Desk drawer, hotel room, the bar that's seen better days
- Danger: romanticizes addiction without consequence
- What it offers: vulnerability, self-medication, ritual
- Subversion: they quit; sobriety is its own kind of exposure

**The Office**
- Frosted glass, worn chair, name on the door (or not)
- Where the case arrives, where the thinking happens
- Danger: becomes mere set dressing
- What it offers: home base, identity marker, the space of waiting
- Subversion: the office is gone; everything happens in motion

## Style and Voice

### First-Person Narration

Noir lives in first-person. The voice IS the genre.

**Key elements:**
- Wry observation that masks pain
- Simile as worldview ("She had the kind of smile that makes you check for your wallet")
- Present-tense urgency or past-tense reflection (each has different effects)
- Selective detail—notice what matters, dismiss what doesn't
- Interior monologue that reveals more than the speaker intends

**Voice markers:**
- Short sentences for impact. Long ones when thought spirals.
- Understatement of violence and emotion
- Slang of the era or setting (research, don't invent)
- Rhythm matters—read it aloud, it should have cadence

### Prose Texture

**Noir prose tends toward:**
- Economy with strategic expansion
- Physical grounding (the ache in the jaw, the weight of the gun)
- Weather as character
- Architecture as psychology (the building tells you about its owner)
- Light source awareness (where does illumination come from, what stays dark?)

**The noir simile:**
- Unexpected comparisons that illuminate
- Often funny, often bitter, always revealing
- "The room smelled like old money and new fear"
- "She looked at me the way you look at the last cigarette in the pack"
- "His smile had all the warmth of a cancelled check"

**Avoid:**
- Purple prose that loses the hardboiled edge
- Similes that try too hard (becomes self-parody)
- Modern sensibility intruding without purpose
- Narration that tells rather than shows (but noir allows some telling—voice earns it)

### Dialogue Considerations

**Noir dialogue:**
- Says less than it means
- Questions answer questions
- Lies nested in truth
- Rhythm and snap
- Silence loaded

**Example dynamics:**
- Detective and cop: old grudges, professional contempt, occasional necessity
- Detective and femme fatale: attraction and suspicion braided
- Detective and client: lies being test-tasted
- Detective and thug: casual violence lurking in casual words

**What's not said:**
- The pause before the answer
- The deflection that IS the answer
- The subject changed too quickly
- The name not spoken

## Pacing Expectations

### The Unraveling Structure

Noir doesn't build toward solution; it peels toward revelation.

**Classic movement:**
1. **The Hook** — case arrives, usually not what it seems
2. **The Descent** — each answer reveals worse questions
3. **The Complications** — everyone's connected; nothing's coincidence
4. **The Violence** — bodies accumulate, stakes clarify
5. **The Turn** — the real case emerges from behind the presented case
6. **The Revelation** — truth arrives, often too late to matter
7. **The Cost** — resolution extracts its price; surviving isn't the same as winning

### Everyone's Lying

**Layer the deceptions:**
- First lie: what the client tells you
- Second lie: what the client believes
- Third lie: what someone told the client
- Fourth lie: what you tell yourself about the case
- Truth: often uglier than all the lies combined

**Revelation pacing:**
- Dole out information in fragments
- Let the reader/player suspect before confirming
- The thing you learn in chapter two recontextualizes in chapter eight
- Some revelations should make everything worse, not better

### The Reveal That Makes It Worse

**Noir revelations:**
- Rarely restore justice
- Often implicate more than they exonerate
- Sometimes render the whole effort pointless
- Occasionally offer bitter choice instead of clean resolution

**What "solving" the case might mean:**
- The killer's known, but untouchable
- The killer's dead, but the system that created them persists
- The protagonist becomes complicit in covering truth
- Justice is technically served; nothing is actually fixed

## Common Pitfalls

**Parody Without Substance**
- Problem: all fedoras and cigarettes, no actual darkness
- Fix: the style must serve story; if you remove the trappings, something real should remain
- Test: can you describe your noir without mentioning rain, shadows, or booze?

**Misogyny Masquerading as Genre**
- Problem: femme fatale punished for sexuality; women exist only as threats or victims
- Fix: the fatale has her own story, her own reasons; women in noir can be complex without being punished
- Note: historical accuracy isn't excuse; you choose what to replicate

**All Style, No Story**
- Problem: beautiful sentences, atmospheric descriptions, nothing actually happening
- Fix: voice serves narrative; every stylistic choice should advance something
- Test: does each scene change something? (Information, relationship, situation?)

**Nihilism Without Stakes**
- Problem: if nothing matters, why should we care?
- Fix: noir protagonists care—despite everything, something still matters to them
- The code exists precisely because the world doesn't care

**The Twist That Explains Too Much**
- Problem: everything is connected in ways that strain credibility
- Fix: some things are coincidence; not everyone is related to the case
- Reality is messier than conspiracy

**Violence Without Weight**
- Problem: bodies stack up without consequence or feeling
- Fix: death should land; even hardened protagonists register the cost
- The seventh corpse should mean something

**The Detective Who's Always Right**
- Problem: protagonist cracks every code, reads every person perfectly
- Fix: let them be wrong; let misreads cost them
- The best noir detectives are often wrong about themselves

## Atmosphere Design

### Building Noir Feel

**Environmental elements:**
- Time of day matters (noir lives after midnight, in the small hours)
- Weather as metaphor (not just rain—heat, fog, the stillness before storms)
- Light sources defined (neon, streetlamp, the glow of a cigarette)
- Sound palette (traffic, distant sirens, jazz from somewhere, footsteps echoing)
- Architecture as character (buildings have moods, neighborhoods have histories)

**Sensory palette:**
- **Sight:** chiaroscuro—where light meets dark, what's half-seen
- **Sound:** what shouldn't be audible at 3 AM, silence with weight
- **Smell:** cigarette smoke, cheap perfume, gun oil, old whiskey, rain on hot pavement
- **Touch:** the weight of a gun, a punch that lands, skin warmer than expected
- **Taste:** whiskey burning, blood in the mouth, the bitter at the bottom of the coffee cup

### The City as Character

**Urban noir requires:**
- Named locations with personality
- Parts of town that mean things (money, danger, vice, desperation)
- History embedded in streets (the building where it happened, the bar that closed)
- The city at night vs. the city's daytime mask

**The city's layers:**
- Surface: what tourists see, what the papers report
- Street level: what locals know, what cops ignore
- Underneath: who really runs things, where the bodies are buried

### Interior Spaces

**Offices:** tell us about their occupants
- What's on the desk, what's in the drawer
- How much light gets in
- What the window looks at

**Apartments:** tell us about lives
- The bottle by the bed, the photo turned face-down
- What's clean, what's neglected
- Evidence of who else has been there

**Bars:** tell us about need
- Who drinks here and when
- What the bartender knows
- The booth in the back, the stool by the door

## Character Depth Extensions

Genre-specific entity fields for character files. The calibrator adds these sections based on active genre modules.

### Extraction Questions

**Vice Architecture:**
- "What's their specific vice? Booze? Gambling? Sex? Danger? Violence? Something stranger?"
- "How do they manage it—or fail to? Functional addict? Barely hanging on? Denial?"
- "What's the ritual of indulgence? The bottle in the desk drawer? The back-alley game? The call they shouldn't make?"
- "What does the vice give them? Numbness? Control? Punishment? Pleasure they think they don't deserve?"

**Moral Compromise:**
- "What's the line between necessary evil and corruption? Where is it for them?"
- "What have they done that they can't undo? What choice haunts them?"
- "How do they justify the dirty work? 'It's the job'? 'Someone has to'? Do they even justify anymore?"
- "What would redeem them? Or is redemption off the table? Do they even want it?"

**Cynicism Structure:**
- "What idealism is buried underneath the cynicism? What did they used to believe?"
- "What still makes them angry? If everything's corrupt, why does anything matter?"
- "What do they pretend not to care about that they actually care about deeply?"
- "Who they were before vs. who they are now—what's the distance? Can they still see that person?"

**Corruption Tolerance:**
- "How dirty will they get? What laws will they break? What ethics will they bend?"
- "Who do they protect despite themselves? Who do they choose over the job?"
- "When do they draw the line and say 'not this, not here'? What's still sacred?"
- "What corruption have they accepted as the cost of doing business? What still disgusts them?"

**Loyalty Tests:**
- "What person or principle won't they betray? What's the one unbreakable thing?"
- "What would it take to break that loyalty? Money? Threat? Seeing them revealed as false?"
- "Who's tested them? How did they respond? Did they surprise themselves?"
- "If pushed to choose between loyalty and survival, what wins? Between loyalty and justice?"

**Damage Markers (from original voice section):**
- "What broke them? One moment or slow erosion?"
- "How have they adapted around the wound? What's the scar tissue?"
- "When does it show despite their control? What reveals the damage underneath?"

**Code Elements:**
- "What lines won't they cross? (The stated code.)"
- "What lines do they TELL THEMSELVES they won't cross? (The lie they need.)"
- "What would make them cross anyway? (The truth about their limits.)"

**Cynicism Calibration:**
- "How do they deflect with humor? What's their defense mechanism style?"
- "What still reaches them past the armor? What can't they be cynical about?"
- "Who were they before? What vestige of that earlier self remains?"

## Genre Integration Notes

### Tech Noir / Cyberpunk Noir

**Synthesis:**
- Neon replaces streetlight, same shadows
- Corporate power replaces political machines, same corruption
- Digital surveillance adds paranoia layers
- The future is still rain-slicked; the cynicism compiles

**Key adjustments:**
- Hacking as investigation method
- AI as femme fatale variant (who programs the manipulation?)
- Memory modification complicates truth (what do you really remember?)
- Corporate immunity as ultimate corruption

**Extract to:**
- `setting.yaml` → Add tech level, surveillance systems, corporate hierarchy
- `entities.yaml` → Add digital identity, implant dependencies, data as currency

### Fantasy Noir

**Synthesis:**
- Magic as power system (who controls it, who's excluded)
- Species politics as class/race dynamics
- Divination complicates investigation (can you scry truth?)
- Curses as noir past (what follows you)

**Key adjustments:**
- Magic has costs, or it undermines noir stakes
- Establish what magic cannot do (limits create tension)
- The fantastic should deepen, not diminish, moral complexity
- Gods as ultimate corrupt authorities?

**Extract to:**
- `setting.yaml` → Magic system limits, species hierarchy, metaphysical rules
- `entities.yaml` → Magical capabilities, supernatural debts, inhuman tells

### Historical Noir

**Synthesis:**
- Period research grounds the world
- Historical events as backdrop to personal corruption
- Era-specific power structures (who was untouchable when?)
- Language/dialogue requires care (not museum piece, but not anachronistic)

**Key adjustments:**
- Don't let history lesson overwhelm story
- What was considered acceptable/legal then that readers now recognize as corrupt?
- Historical marginalization adds noir stakes for certain characters
- Period atmosphere isn't just costume—it's limitation and opportunity

### Noir + Horror

**Synthesis:**
- Cosmic indifference as ultimate noir fatalism
- The corruption goes all the way down (literally)
- Investigating something that will destroy you to understand
- The femme fatale might not be human

**Key adjustments:**
- Horror adds stakes noir usually lacks (cosmic, not just personal)
- Maintaining noir voice when confronting the supernatural
- The protagonist's code vs. forces that make ethics irrelevant
- Survival might be worse than death

## Final Notes

Noir earns its cynicism. The world depicted has made the protagonist this way; their bitterness is evidence, not affectation. The genre asks: what do you do when the game is rigged, when justice is a story the powerful tell, when everyone has an angle—including you?

The answer noir gives: you develop a code. You follow it. You probably lose anyway. But there's something in the losing that matters more than winning would have.

The rain keeps falling. The cigarette burns down. The dame had an angle you didn't see. But you're still standing in the doorway, and someone still needs to ask the questions no one wants answered.

That's the job.
