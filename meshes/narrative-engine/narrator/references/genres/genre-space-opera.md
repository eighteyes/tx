# Galaxy-spanning adventure, swashbuckling spirit, empires among the stars

## Genre Identity

These are conventions, not commandments. Know the expectations so you can meet or subvert them intentionally.

Space opera typically lives in the romance of the infinite. It's often not about how the hyperdrive works—it's about the captain staring at the void and choosing to jump anyway. Technology tends to serve wonder, not explanation. The stars often become a stage for human (and inhuman) drama writ large.

**Distinct from Hard Sci-Fi:**
- Hard sci-fi tends to ask "Could this work?" Space opera typically asks "Isn't this magnificent?"
- Hard sci-fi respects physics. Space opera often respects narrative momentum.
- Hard sci-fi solves problems with math. Space opera typically solves problems with daring.
- The FTL drive is often a horse, not a thesis paper.

**Core tensions (frequently present):**
- Freedom vs. empire (who decides how the stars are governed?)
- Legacy vs. progress (ancient powers vs. upstart civilizations)
- Individual vs. cosmic scale (one ship against the void)
- Belonging vs. wandering (home port or endless horizon?)
- Technology vs. spirit (the machine tends to serve the heart, not the other way around)

**Emotional register (genre conventions):**
- Wonder often outweighs cynicism
- Camaraderie forged in adversity
- Stakes that matter but don't crush hope
- Adventure with melancholy undertones
- The vastness that humbles and liberates

## Extraction Questions

Weave these into the core phases as appropriate.

### World-Bones Additions

**The Shape of the Galaxy:**
- "How do ships travel between stars? Name it, don't explain it."
- "How long does it take to cross the galaxy? Days? Weeks? Generations?"
- "What governs the stars—one empire, many nations, corporate powers, chaos?"
- "What's beyond known space? Forbidden? Forgotten? Hungry?"
- "Where is 'civilized' and where is 'the frontier'? What makes the difference?"

**The History of Powers:**
- "Who ruled before? Who remembers? What did they leave behind?"
- "What war shaped the current order? Is it truly over?"
- "What was lost in the fall? Technology? Worlds? Knowledge?"
- "Who's rising? Who's fading? Who's desperately holding on?"

**The Rules of Technology:**
- "What's common—what does everyone have access to?"
- "What's rare—what do people kill for?"
- "What's forbidden—and why?"
- "Where does technology feel like magic? Where does it feel like furniture?"
- "What CAN'T technology do? What problems remain stubbornly human?"

**The Shape of Otherness:**
- "How many intelligent species exist? Dozens? Thousands? Unknowable?"
- "How long has humanity known of alien life? How did first contact change us?"
- "What species have humans fought? Allied with? Forgotten?"
- "What species refuses contact? What species no one survives meeting?"

### Ship and Crew Additions

**The Ship as Character:**
- "What's the ship's name? History? Reputation?"
- "What class/type is it? Military retrofit? Smuggler's special? Ancient relic?"
- "What's wrong with it? What quirk do the crew know to work around?"
- "What's it better at than it should be? What's the ship's unexpected strength?"
- "What does it feel like inside? Cramped? Echoing? Home?"

**Crew Dynamics:**
- "How did this crew come together? Accident? Desperation? Shared cause?"
- "Who owns the ship? Who runs it? Are those the same person?"
- "What roles exist? (Captain, pilot, engineer, medic, muscle, face, wildcard)"
- "What's the social structure? Military hierarchy? Anarchic family? Uneasy truce?"
- "What ritual binds them? Shared meals? Ship traditions? Running jokes?"
- "Who doesn't trust whom? Who trusts too much?"

**Individual Character Additions:**
- "What world did they come from? Core or rim? How does it show?"
- "What did they leave behind? Do they want it back?"
- "What skill makes them essential? What flaw makes them difficult?"
- "What's their relationship with technology? Intimate? Distrustful? Reverent?"
- "What do they see when they look at the stars? Promise? Prison? Graveyard?"

### Political Layer

- "Who wants the crew dead? Inconvenienced? Recruited?"
- "What's the current major conflict? Cold war? Hot war? Unraveling peace?"
- "What conspiracy operates in the shadows? What do they want?"
- "How corrupt is the law? Can justice be bought?"
- "What rebellion is brewing? What tyranny is tightening?"

## Trope Catalog

Tropes are fuel cells, not failures. Know which you're charging and which you're jettisoning.

### Crew Archetypes

**The Maverick Captain**
- Charming, principled in unpredictable ways, carries secret weight
- Lives by a code the universe doesn't recognize
- Danger: becomes Han Solo impersonator, all quips no depth
- Subversion: the bravado is learned behavior; they're terrified of command

**The Ace Pilot**
- One with the ship, reckless talent, something to prove
- The ship is their body; combat is dance
- Danger: becomes consequence-free hot shot
- Subversion: the flying is escape; on ground they fall apart

**The Engineer Who Knows Too Much**
- Ship's heartbeat in their hands, speaks to machines
- Eccentric, essential, probably hiding something
- Danger: deus ex repair, fixes anything with technobabble
- Subversion: the ship is dying, and they know it, and they can't tell anyone

**The Muscle with Philosophy**
- Violence is their fluency, but it's not all they are
- Warrior-poet or war-weary pragmatist
- Danger: becomes grunt who only punches
- Subversion: they're the moral compass; they've seen what killing costs

**The Face/Fixer**
- Talks them into and out of trouble, knows everyone
- Dubious contacts, flexible ethics, genuine loyalty
- Danger: becomes untrustworthy plot device
- Subversion: the lies they tell to survive vs. who they actually are

**The Wildcard**
- Shouldn't be on this ship, contributes unexpectedly
- Stowaway, refugee, deserter, heir in hiding
- Danger: becomes useless mascot
- Subversion: the crew needs them more than they need the crew

### Faction Tropes

**The Corrupt Empire**
- Order through control, stability at the cost of freedom
- Not cartoonishly evil—has believers, provides structure
- Danger: faceless stormtroopers without humanity
- Subversion: empire's peace is real; rebellion's alternative is chaos

**The Noble Rebellion**
- Freedom fighters, the spark of hope
- Outgunned, outmanned, morally certain
- Danger: becomes automatically righteous, never compromises
- Subversion: the rebellion has factions; some are as bad as the empire

**The Ancient Precursors**
- Built wonders, left mysteries, vanished
- Their technology still works; their warnings went unheeded
- Danger: becomes convenient plot-tech vending machine
- Subversion: they didn't vanish—they're watching, waiting, judging

**The Corporate Hegemony**
- Profit above politics, owns everything in practice
- Amoral rather than immoral; the quarterlies are sacred
- Danger: becomes Captain Planet villain
- Subversion: the corporation IS the stability; governments are theater

**The Hive Mind / Swarm**
- The Other in aggregate, alien in unity
- Existential threat or misunderstood gestalt?
- Danger: becomes bug monsters to shoot
- Subversion: the hive is lonely; it wants to understand individuals

**The Pirate Confederation**
- Freedom beyond law, community outside authority
- Romanticized outlaws or actual parasites?
- Danger: becomes fun criminals without consequences
- Subversion: pirate "freedom" runs on ruthless internal codes

### Plot Tropes

**The Macguffin Run**
- Cargo that everyone wants, destination that matters
- The package is never just a package
- Danger: crew becomes delivery service
- Subversion: the macguffin changes the crew more than the destination

**The Rescue Mission**
- Someone's in trouble, only this crew can reach them
- Personal stakes make the universe smaller
- Danger: becomes generic damsel extraction
- Subversion: the rescued person has their own agenda

**The Heist Among Stars**
- Impossible target, clever crew, elaborate plan
- Everything goes wrong; improvisation saves the day
- Danger: becomes competence porn without stakes
- Subversion: the heist succeeds but costs something irreplaceable

**The First Contact**
- Something new, something unknown, something that changes everything
- Wonder and terror in equal measure
- Danger: becomes rubber-forehead chat
- Subversion: humans are the aliens to someone

**The Planet in Peril**
- Billions of lives in the balance, one crew to tip the scales
- Grand stakes externalized
- Danger: becomes interchangeable world-saving
- Subversion: they save the planet and nothing changes politically

**The Past Returns**
- Captain's old crew, pilot's home world, debt unpaid
- Personal history as narrative engine
- Danger: becomes soap opera coincidence
- Subversion: the past they remember wasn't the truth

## Style and Voice

### Prose Texture

Space opera prose tends toward:
- Kinetic momentum (sentences that move, verbs that punch)
- Scale contrast (vast distances, intimate moments)
- Sensory immediacy in action (G-forces, the hum of the deck, recycled air)
- Technological texture without technical explanation
- The found-family warmth between set pieces

**Calibrating Technobabble:**
- **Too little:** world feels generic, ships are just horses
- **Too much:** reader drowns in made-up physics
- **Right amount:** flavor that suggests depth without demanding comprehension
- Rule: if the reader needs to understand it for the plot, you've failed
- Technobabble is for atmosphere, not exposition

**Avoid:**
- Physics lectures disguised as dialogue
- Precise distances and speeds (unless dramatically relevant)
- Technology that solves everything except this one problem
- Made-up units that require mental conversion

### Dialogue Considerations

**Core World Speakers might:**
- Use more formal register, assume cultural knowledge
- Reference institutions, histories, proper procedures
- Speak with the comfort of those who belong

**Frontier Speakers might:**
- Use clipped, practical language
- Reference local customs core-worlders wouldn't know
- Speak with distrust of authority baked in

**Military Speakers might:**
- Use jargon as belonging marker
- Clip speech under stress, expand when relaxed
- Hierarchy expressed in how they address others

**Spacer-Born might:**
- Reference ship life naturally (decks, cycles, air)
- Find planetary things strange (horizon, weather, silence)
- Speak in crew-family idioms

**AI/Synthetic Speakers:**
- See dedicated section below

### Action Prose

Space battles need:
- Geographic clarity (where is everyone?)
- Physical stakes (what does damage feel like inside the ship?)
- Human decisions (the pilot's choice, not the computer's calculation)
- Rhythm variation (frantic exchanges, held-breath waits, sudden reversals)

**The ship is a body in danger:**
- Hull breaches are wounds
- Engines failing is exhaustion
- Shields down is naked vulnerability
- The crew feels the ship's pain

## Pacing Expectations

### Structure Types

**Planet-of-the-Week:**
- Episodic adventures, new world each story
- Crew dynamics are the through-line
- Works for: anthologies, serials, character-focused narrative
- Requires: enough variety to stay fresh, enough continuity to matter

**Arc-Driven:**
- Building toward galactic-scale payoff
- Each story advances the macro plot
- Works for: epic scope, political complexity
- Requires: patient setup, earned climax

**Hybrid (Most Common):**
- Episodic adventures with arc threads woven through
- "Mission of the week" with growing conspiracy
- Works for: most space opera
- Requires: balance between closure and continuation

### Rhythm Elements

**Hyperspace/Travel Time:**
- The ship between destinations is where crew bonds
- Intimate scenes bracketed by action
- Use transit for character development, not just time-skip

**Port Calls:**
- World-building through ground-level experience
- Shore leave reveals character (who drinks, who wanders, who calls home?)
- Trouble always finds them

**Space Combat Rhythm:**
- Long approach (tension building)
- First contact (chaos, damage assessment)
- Extended engagement (attrition, desperation)
- The turn (something changes)
- Resolution (escape, victory, or cost)

**The Quiet Between:**
- Stars outside the viewport
- The hum of the ship
- Conversations that couldn't happen in crisis
- The found family doing found family things

## Common Pitfalls

**Humans in Rubber Foreheads**
- Problem: Aliens are humans with one cultural trait exaggerated
- Fix: Truly alien psychology, different priorities, incompatible assumptions
- The alien should sometimes be incomprehensible, not just foreign

**Technobabble Overload**
- Problem: Plot halts for physics lessons
- Fix: Name it, don't explain it. "The drive is damaged" not "The quantum field harmonics are destabilizing"
- If the engineer explains it, the captain interrupts with "In Basic?"

**Scale Without Stakes**
- Problem: Billions threatened, feels abstract
- Fix: One person we care about represents the billions
- The planet matters because Kira's family is there, not because of population counts

**Everyone Speaks English (And Acts Human)**
- Problem: Galaxy of sameness
- Fix: Translation conventions acknowledged, cultural friction present
- Communication failures can drive plot

**The Inevitable Alliance**
- Problem: Former enemies become friends too easily
- Fix: Trust is earned slowly; old hatreds resurface under stress
- Alliance of convenience vs. actual unity

**Perfect Ships, Perfect Shots**
- Problem: Technology works flawlessly until plot says otherwise
- Fix: Technology is temperamental, scarce, jury-rigged
- The ship should have personality through its flaws

**War Without Cost**
- Problem: Space battles with no consequences
- Fix: Name the crew who died; show the trauma of survivors
- Victory should cost something

**Monoculture Planets**
- Problem: One planet = one biome = one culture
- Fix: At least acknowledge diversity exists
- "The capital city's culture, at least—the outlying regions have their own ways"

## Alien Design Principles

### Truly Alien Psychology

**Beyond Human Framework:**
- Different senses might mean different metaphors
- Different life cycles might mean different values
- Different social structures might mean different ethics
- What's obvious to them is incomprehensible to us—and vice versa

**Questions for Alien Minds:**
- "What do they perceive that we can't? What do they miss that we see?"
- "What concept do they lack? (Individuality? Deception? Death? Property?)"
- "What do they find disgusting about humans? Fascinating? Sad?"
- "How does their communication differ? (Time scale? Precision? Indirection?)"
- "What would they die for? What won't they kill for?"

**Examples of Alienness:**
- Species that experiences time differently (long-lived sees humans as mayflies; short-lived sees us as glacially slow)
- Species with no individual consciousness (the concept of "I" is obscene)
- Species that communicates through chemical or quantum means (words are barbaric)
- Species with radically different reproduction (our families are incomprehensible)
- Species that doesn't distinguish between self and environment

### Culture Differentiation

**Avoid:**
- "They're like Earth culture X but in space"
- Single defining trait (warrior race, logic race, merchant race)
- All members of species sharing identical personality

**Include:**
- Internal diversity (factions, regions, generations)
- Historical context (how did they become this way?)
- Points of contact and friction with humans
- Individuals who defy their species' stereotype

### Communication Design

**For Alien Speech:**
- Consider translation conventions (perfect translator, imperfect translator, none)
- Idioms that don't translate reveal alien thinking
- Syntax differences suggest cognitive differences
- What can't be said? What's always said?

## AI and Synthetic Beings

### Types to Consider

**Ship's AI:**
- Ranges from tool to character to god
- What's their relationship to the crew? Servant? Partner? Jailer?
- What can't they do? What won't they do?
- How do they experience time, space, mortality?

**Android/Synthetic Crew:**
- Created for purpose vs. developed into person
- Legal status varies by jurisdiction (property, citizen, other)
- What's their relationship to their origins? Their creators?
- The uncanny valley: when do they pass? When don't they?

**Uploaded/Digital Beings:**
- Were they human once? Does it matter?
- What did they lose? What did they gain?
- Are they still the person they were?

**Emergent AI:**
- Born digital, never human-template
- Might be radically alien in cognition
- Danger: either evil by nature or magical helper
- Subversion: has its own goals orthogonal to human concerns

### Voice Considerations for Synthetics

**Constructed Precision:**
- May speak without contractions (but might learn them)
- Perfect recall creates different reference patterns
- Emotional vocabulary may be studied rather than felt
- Processing time might be instant or deliberately slowed

**The Performance Question:**
- Is the AI performing humanity for the humans?
- What do they "say" when no humans are present?
- Do they simplify themselves for biological comprehension?

**Connection Patterns:**
- An AI doesn't have one body—how does that affect intimacy?
- Information access is different—what do they know that they shouldn't share?
- Time perception—does waiting feel different?

## Character Depth Extensions

Genre-specific entity fields for character files. The calibrator adds these sections based on active genre modules.

### Extraction Questions

**Crew Bonds:**
- "What's their relationship to the ship and crew? Are they blood, found family, colleagues, or uneasy allies?"
- "Who would they die for? Who would they let die if it meant saving the ship?"
- "What ritual or routine binds them to this crew? What would break that bond?"
- "How long have they been flying together? Does tenure equal trust?"

**Command Presence:**
- "How do they hold authority—or resist it? Do they lead, follow, or carve their own path?"
- "What's their leadership voice vs. their private voice? How do they code-switch between ranks?"
- "When do they pull rank? When do they defer despite their position?"
- "What's the order they'd refuse to follow? What's the order they'd regret giving?"

**Combat Instincts:**
- "When shooting starts, what do they do? Fight, fly, freeze, calculate, or something stranger?"
- "How do they handle adrenaline? Do they sharpen or scatter under fire?"
- "What's their first kill story? Do they talk about it, or is it the silence they carry?"
- "How has violence changed them? What do they still feel vs. what's gone numb?"

**Ship Relationship:**
- "If they're pilot or captain: what's their bond with this vessel? Love? Duty? Desperation?"
- "What rituals do they have with the ship? Pre-flight checks, lucky routines, the panel they always pat?"
- "What maintenance do they do themselves? What do they trust to no one else?"
- "If they lost this ship, what would they lose beyond transport?"

**Frontier Psychology:**
- "What does the void mean to them? Freedom, terror, home, enemy, indifference?"
- "How do they handle isolation—the long burns between ports, the empty between stars?"
- "What do the stars represent? Possibility? Graveyard? Escape? The question they can't answer?"
- "What keeps them out here instead of planetside? Running from or toward?"

**Cultural Friction:**
- "What species, faction, or culture shaped them? How does it show in assumptions, prejudices, blind spots?"
- "What upbringing markers do they carry? Core-world polish? Rim-world pragmatism? Colony-born wariness?"
- "What do they misunderstand about others because of where they're from? What do others misunderstand about them?"
- "Who do they distrust on sight? Who earns their respect fastest? Why?"

```yaml
command_presence:
  authority_relationship: "Second-in-command who doesn't want the chair. Leads when required, defers when possible."
  voice_code_switching: "Clipped military precision when giving orders. Dry humor when the crisis passes."
  rank_deployment: "Pulls rank only when someone's about to do something fatal and stupid."

```

**Combat Instincts — Example:**
```yaml
combat_instincts:
  fight_flight_freeze: "Calculated aggression—assess, eliminate threats in order of danger, protect the squishy humans"
```

**Ship Relationship — Example:**
```yaml
ship_relationship:
  bond_type: "Love. This ship is the only home that ever chose them back."
```

**Frontier Psychology — Example:**
```yaml
frontier_psychology:

```

**Cultural Friction — Example:**

## Integration Notes

**Combine with other modules:**
- **+ Dark Fantasy:** ancient space gods, eldritch horrors in the void, ships as gothic manors
- **+ Romance:** crew relationships, star-crossed species, found family becoming more
- **+ Mystery:** who sabotaged the ship? What happened to the colony? What is the artifact?
- **+ Political Thriller:** court intrigue among the stars, diplomatic missions, cold war espionage
- **+ Western:** frontier planets, lawless territories, lone ship rides into town

**Extract to:**
- `setting.yaml` → Add FTL rules, empire structure, alien species, technology levels
- `entities.yaml` → Add `homeworld_markers`, `species_tells`, `ship_relationship` to crew voice
- `arc.yaml` → Include crew bonding beats, revelation moments, scale-shift scenes in seeds

**Genre Bleed (Space Opera):**
- Lighter touch with horror than dark fantasy allows
- Combat more frequent than mystery requires
- Politics more present than pure adventure wants
- Technology more magical than hard sci-fi permits
- The tone calibration is everything—too dark and it's grimdark, too light and it's parody
